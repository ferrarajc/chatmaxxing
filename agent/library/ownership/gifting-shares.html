Gifting shares to another person

Section: Ownership Changes & Life Events · Page ID: gifting-shares

How clients give fund shares to someone else, tax considerations, and the paperwork.

On this page

  • How it works
  • Requirements
  • Taxes
  • IRAs
  • Gifts to charity
  • Example

How it works

A client can transfer shares from their taxable account to another person's taxable account (at Bob's) in kind. The recipient receives the shares with the giver's cost basis (carryover basis), with special rules if the value has fallen below basis.

Requirements

  • The recipient must have, or open, an account at Bob's.
  • The giver completes the Gift/Transfer of Shares form, with a medallion signature guarantee.
  • For gifts to a minor, the recipient account is a custodial account. See Custodial accounts (UTMA/UGMA).

Taxes

Gifts are not taxable to the recipient. The giver may need to file a gift tax return if gifts to one person exceed the annual exclusion ($19,000 per recipient for 2025 and 2026), though tax is rarely owed. Refer details to a tax advisor.

IRAs

IRA assets cannot be gifted. A client would have to take a taxable distribution first.

Gifts to charity

Clients can donate shares from a taxable account to a charity's brokerage account, typically to avoid capital gains on appreciated shares. The charity provides its account details; the client completes the transfer form. For IRA giving, see Qualified charitable distributions (QCDs).

Example

A grandfather gives 50 shares of BF500 from his taxable account to his granddaughter's custodial account. The shares move in kind with his cost basis. He completes the gift form with a medallion signature guarantee.
