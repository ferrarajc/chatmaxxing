When a custodial account's minor comes of age

Section: Ownership Changes & Life Events · Page ID: custodial-age-of-majority

What happens when a UTMA/UGMA beneficiary reaches the age of termination, and the custodian's obligations.

On this page

  • The rule
  • Process
  • If the custodian does nothing
  • Custodian concerns
  • Custodial Roth IRAs
  • Example

The rule

A custodial account must be turned over to the beneficiary when they reach the age of termination set by the account's state law (commonly 18 or 21; some states allow the custodian to elect up to 25 when the account is created).

Process

  1. About 60 days before the termination age, we notify the custodian and the beneficiary.
  2. The beneficiary opens an individual account in their own name (or we convert the account), verifying their identity.
  3. The assets move to the beneficiary's account in kind.
  4. The custodian's access ends.

If the custodian does nothing

The obligation exists regardless. Once the beneficiary reaches the termination age, the beneficiary can provide proof of age and identity and request the transfer.

Custodian concerns

Custodians sometimes worry about a young adult receiving the money. The gift is irrevocable; the account legally belongs to the beneficiary. Custodians cannot extend control beyond the termination age. Explain kindly and factually.

Custodial Roth IRAs

When the owner of a custodial Roth IRA reaches the age of majority, the custodian's role ends and the owner takes control of their own Roth IRA.

Example

A UTMA account under Pennsylvania law terminates at 21. When the beneficiary turns 21, we notify the custodian and the young adult, who opens an individual account and receives the shares.
