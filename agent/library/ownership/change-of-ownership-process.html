The change-of-ownership process

Section: Ownership Changes & Life Events · Page ID: change-of-ownership-process

The general steps for any change of ownership: death, divorce, trust, or gift, and who handles each.

On this page

  • When it applies
  • General steps
  • In chat
  • Example

When it applies

Any time assets move from one owner or registration to another:

  • Death of an account holder (Estate Services). See When someone reports a death.
  • Divorce (Account Services). See Divorce and account transfers.
  • Transfer to or between trusts (Account Services). See Retitling accounts to a trust.
  • Gifts of shares (Account Services). See Gifting shares to another person.
  • Custodial termination (Account Services). See When a custodial account's minor comes of age.
  • Legal name change, which is not a change of ownership but uses similar paperwork. See Legal name changes.

General steps

  1. The client or claimant contacts us.
  2. We identify the type of change and send a personalized document checklist.
  3. The client submits documents (secure upload, mail).
  4. The responsible team reviews; we may ask for missing items.
  5. Assets are transferred, usually 5–10 business days after documents are complete.
  6. Written confirmation is sent.

In chat

Your job is to identify the type of change, explain the general process, send the right form, and route to the responsible team, with a clear next step for the client.

Example

A client says, "My mother died, and my brother and I are her beneficiaries." That is a death-related change handled by Estate Services. A client who says, "I want to put my account in my trust" is handled by Account Services. Identifying the type early sends the client to the right team the first time.
