Retitling accounts to a trust

Section: Ownership Changes & Life Events · Page ID: trust-reregistration

Moving taxable accounts into a revocable living trust, changing trustees, and what can't go into a trust.

Moving an account into a trust

When a client creates a revocable living trust, their estate attorney may advise retitling taxable accounts in the trust's name. The process:

  1. Open a trust account at Bob's (see Opening trust accounts).
  2. Complete the transfer form to move shares in kind from the individual (or joint) account to the trust account.
  3. Account Services reviews the trust certification.

For a revocable trust using the grantor's SSN, this is not a taxable event, and the cost basis carries over.

IRAs can't be retitled

An IRA must be owned by an individual. To coordinate an IRA with a trust, the client names the trust as beneficiary. See Trusts, estates, and charities as beneficiaries.

Changing trustees

When a trustee dies, resigns, or is added, Account Services needs:

  • An updated Certification of Trust or the trust amendment naming the current trustees.
  • A death certificate (if a trustee died).
  • ID for any new trustee.

In chat

Explain the steps and route to Account Services. Questions about whether to use a trust go to an estate planning attorney.

Example

A couple creates the "Patel Family Revocable Trust" and wants their joint account in it. They open a trust account at Bob's, provide the Certification of Trust, and transfer the shares in kind. Their SSN remains the tax ID, so nothing is taxable.
