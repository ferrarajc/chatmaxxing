Selling in IRAs and taking money out

Section: Selling · Page ID: selling-in-iras

The difference between selling inside an IRA (no tax) and withdrawing from an IRA (a distribution), and how to handle each in chat.

On this page

  • Selling inside the IRA
  • Taking money out
  • Combining the two
  • Moving IRA money to another IRA

Selling inside the IRA

When a client sells a fund in an IRA and keeps the proceeds in the IRA as cash, or exchanges into another fund, nothing is taxed. There is no 1099-B for IRA trades. Clients sometimes worry that selling in their IRA triggers taxes; reassure them it does not.

Taking money out

Moving money from an IRA to the client's bank or a check is a distribution. Distributions from a Traditional or SEP IRA are taxable income, may be subject to a 10% penalty before age 59½, and have federal withholding of 10% by default. Roth distributions follow different rules. Use the Request a Distribution task, not the Sell task, when the client wants the money out. See Procedure: Request a Distribution (chat) and IRA distributions overview.

Combining the two

If the IRA holds no cash, a distribution requires selling shares first. The distribution task handles this: the client says how much they want and from which account, and shares are sold as needed. If the client wants a specific fund sold for the distribution, note it.

Moving IRA money to another IRA

Transferring to another IRA custodian is neither a sale for tax purposes nor a distribution if done as a direct transfer. See IRA-to-IRA transfers.
