Record, ex-dividend, reinvestment, and pay dates

Section: Dividends & Fund Distributions · Page ID: ex-dividend-dates

The dates in a distribution, which one determines who gets paid, and why the NAV drops.

On this page

  • The dates
  • Who gets paid
  • The NAV drop
  • Explaining it

The dates

DateMeaning
Record dateShareholders on the fund's records at this date receive the distribution
Ex-dividend dateThe date the NAV is reduced by the distribution amount. For mutual funds, usually the record date or the next business day
Reinvestment dateThe date DRIP shares are purchased, usually the ex-date
Payable dateThe date cash distributions are paid

Who gets paid

A client who owns shares on the record date receives the distribution. A client who buys on or after the ex-dividend date does not receive that distribution. A client who sells on or after the ex-date still receives it.

The NAV drop

On the ex-dividend date, the NAV falls by the amount distributed, in addition to any market movement. Example: BF500 NAV is $220.00; it distributes $1.636; on the ex-date, before market movement, the NAV is $218.364. The shareholder now has $218.364 per share plus $1.636 per share in cash or reinvested shares: still $220.00.

Explaining it

Clients who see a drop on a distribution date may think they lost money. "The fund paid a dividend of $1.64 per share that day, so the share price dropped by that amount. You received the dividend, reinvested as additional shares, so your total value wasn't reduced by the payment." See "Why did my fund go down?".
