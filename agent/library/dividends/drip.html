Dividend reinvestment (DRIP)

Section: Dividends & Fund Distributions · Page ID: drip

How automatic reinvestment works, the default setting, taxes on reinvested dividends, and how to change it.

On this page

  • How it works
  • Default
  • Changing the setting
  • Taxes
  • Common questions

How it works

With dividend reinvestment on, each distribution is used to buy more shares of the same fund, at the NAV on the reinvestment date, with no fee. Example: a client holds 500 shares of BF500; the fund pays $0.45 per share ($225); at a NAV of $218.40, DRIP buys 1.030 additional shares.

Default

Reinvestment is on by default for every fund in every new account. Clients can turn it off per fund.

Changing the setting

  • Online: My Account → Dividend Preferences, per fund and account.
  • Chat: the Change Dividend Reinvestment task. See Procedure: Change Dividend Reinvestment (chat).

A change applies to distributions with a record date after the change is processed. If a distribution's record date has already passed, that payment follows the old setting.

Taxes

In a taxable account, reinvested distributions are taxable in the year paid, exactly like cash distributions. Each reinvestment adds to the client's cost basis, which we track and report. In IRAs, reinvested distributions are not taxed. See How fund distributions are taxed.

Common questions

"Why did I get a 1099-DIV if I never received cash?"

Because reinvested dividends are taxable in a taxable account. The reinvested amount increased their cost basis, so they will not be taxed on it again when they sell.

"Can I reinvest dividends from one fund into a different fund?"

Online, clients can direct distributions from one fund into another fund in the same account (cross-reinvestment) at My Account → Dividend Preferences. The chat task only turns same-fund reinvestment on or off.
