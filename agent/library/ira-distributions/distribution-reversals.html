Undoing a distribution

Section: IRA Distributions · Page ID: distribution-reversals

Whether a client can put money back after an IRA distribution, the 60-day rollover option, and its limits.

On this page

  • The 60-day rollover
  • How to process
  • Our errors

The 60-day rollover

A client who takes a distribution from an IRA and changes their mind can redeposit it into an IRA within 60 days of receiving it, as a rollover. If done in time, the distribution is not taxable.

Limits:

  • Only one IRA-to-IRA indirect rollover is allowed in any 12-month period, across all of the client's IRAs. See The one-rollover-per-year rule.
  • Any tax withheld must be made up from other funds to roll over the full amount; otherwise the withheld portion is taxable.
  • RMDs cannot be rolled over.
  • Inherited IRA distributions cannot be rolled over (except by a spouse treating it as their own).

How to process

The client deposits the money back as a rollover (not a contribution), designated as such. In chat, use the purchase task and make sure the deposit is coded as a rollover; or route to Retirement Specialists if unsure. The 1099-R still reports the distribution; the client reports the rollover on their return, and our Form 5498 reports the rollover contribution.

Our errors

If a distribution happened because of our error (for example, the wrong account was used), we reverse it directly without using the client's 60-day rollover. Escalate to your team lead. See Correcting errors (as-of trades).
