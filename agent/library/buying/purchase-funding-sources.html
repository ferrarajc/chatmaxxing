Cash in the account

An account's total value is its uninvested cash plus the market value of its fund holdings. Cash comes from deposits not yet invested, dividends paid in cash, and sale proceeds left in the account. The client info panel and the portal show cash separately (for example "total incl. $877 cash").

When a client says "use the money in my account", check what they mean:

  • If they mean the cash, confirm the cash balance covers the purchase. A $2,000 purchase from cash fails if the account holds only $877 in cash, even if the total value is $4,800.
  • If they mean money that is invested in another fund, that is an exchange, not a cash purchase.

Uninvested cash earns no return in the account. Clients sometimes leave cash by mistake after a deposit; point it out neutrally.

Linked bank

The bank account must be linked and verified. If the client has several, confirm which. Purchases from a bank linked in the last few days are allowed. If the client has no linked bank, they must link one first (see Linking bank accounts) or use a check or wire.

IRAs

Money from a bank into an IRA is a contribution. Cash already in the IRA (from earlier contributions, dividends, or sales) can be invested without counting again toward the limit. See Purchases in IRAs.
