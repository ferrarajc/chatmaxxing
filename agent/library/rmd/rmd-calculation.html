How the RMD is calculated

Section: Required Minimum Distributions · Page ID: rmd-calculation

The formula, the balance used, which table applies, and worked examples.

  • Worked examples

The formula

RMD = account balance on December 31 of the prior year ÷ distribution period (life-expectancy factor) for the client's age at the end of the current year.

The factor comes from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table for most owners. See Uniform Lifetime Table.

Exception: a much younger spouse

If the client's spouse is the sole primary beneficiary for the entire year and is more than 10 years younger, the IRS Joint and Last Survivor table applies, which gives a smaller RMD. Our calculation uses the joint table when the beneficiary designation and dates of birth on file support it. If a client says their spouse is much younger and the RMD seems too high, check the beneficiary designation.

Worked examples

  • Age 75 at year end, December 31 balance $400,000. Factor 24.6. RMD = $400,000 ÷ 24.6 = $16,260.16.
  • Age 80, balance $250,000. Factor 20.2. RMD = $12,376.24.
  • Age 73 (first year), balance $612,000. Factor 26.5. RMD = $23,094.34.

Adjustments to the balance

The prior year-end balance is increased for any rollover or transfer that was in transit over year end (sent from another account before December 31, received after) and for recharacterizations. Retirement Specialists handle these adjustments.

Each IRA calculated separately

The RMD is calculated separately for each IRA. The client can then take the total from any one or more of their IRAs. See Aggregating RMDs across accounts.

What we show

The account page and client info panel show our calculation for each IRA at Bob's. If the client believes it is wrong, check the date of birth and beneficiary on file, and refer to Retirement Specialists for review.
