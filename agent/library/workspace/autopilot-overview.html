Autopilot: what it is and when to use it

Section: Agent Workspace & Tools · Page ID: autopilot-overview

How autopilot carries routine parts of a conversation, its scopes, and the situations where you should never use it.

On this page

  • What autopilot does
  • Scopes
  • When to use autopilot
  • When not to use autopilot

What autopilot does

Autopilot lets the AI send messages in your column on a timer, so routine work continues while you focus on another conversation. When a column is on autopilot, each drafted reply waits through a short countdown before it sends, and the client sees a normal typing indicator. You can pause, edit, or send immediately at any point. See Autopilot controls: pause, resume, exit, send now.

Scopes

Autopilot runs in a scope that defines its job:

  • Task collection (get-intent): identifies the service task the client wants, then a task expert collects the required details one question at a time. When every detail is collected and confirmed, a Proposed Action card appears for your review. See Task experts and the task menu and The Proposed Action card.
  • Callback: arranges a phone callback at a time the client chooses, checking availability. Requests for financial advice are routed here, because a callback can be fulfilled by a licensed professional. See Scheduling callbacks.
  • Idle check: sends a gentle check-in when the client has gone quiet.

The scope recommendation in the AI support panel suggests which scope fits the current moment.

When to use autopilot

  • Collecting routine details for a well-understood task (an automatic investment, a tax form request, a contact update).
  • Scheduling a callback.
  • Keeping a quiet conversation alive while you handle a more urgent column.

When not to use autopilot

  • Bereavement conversations, complaints, fraud reports, or any emotionally charged chat.
  • When the client is confused or the request is ambiguous.
  • When the client is asking for advice, other than routing them to a callback.
  • When you are unsure what the client needs.

Safeguards built in

Autopilot never submits a task on its own. Every task ends in a Proposed Action that you must approve (or, for client-submitted tasks, that the client must approve). Autopilot will not give investment advice, will not discuss topics outside the task, and will tell the client when it cannot help with something. Even so, you remain responsible for every message sent from your column.
