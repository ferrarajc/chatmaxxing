Tax effects of exchanges

Section: Exchanges · Page ID: exchanges-tax

Why exchanges in taxable accounts are taxable sales, and how to explain this without giving tax advice.

On this page

  • Taxable accounts
  • IRAs
  • Wash sales
  • Cost basis method

Taxable accounts

An exchange is treated as a sale of the "from" fund. If the shares are worth more than their cost basis, the client realizes a capital gain; if less, a loss. The exchange appears on Form 1099-B for the year. See Taxes on sales in taxable accounts.

Clients are often surprised by this, because the money stayed invested. Explain it before submitting an exchange in a taxable account if the client seems unaware: "Just so you know, in a taxable account an exchange counts as a sale for tax purposes, so it may result in a capital gain or loss."

IRAs

Exchanges inside a Traditional, Roth, SEP, or inherited IRA have no tax effect.

Wash sales

If an exchange sells shares at a loss and the client buys the same fund (or a "substantially identical" one) within 30 days before or after, part or all of the loss may be disallowed under the wash-sale rule. Whether two different index funds are substantially identical is a tax question for the client's advisor. See The wash-sale rule.

Cost basis method

The cost basis method chosen (average cost by default) determines which shares' basis is used. Clients who want to choose specific lots must set specific identification before the exchange. See Cost basis methods.
