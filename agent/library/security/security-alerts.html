Security alerts clients receive

Section: Identity, Privacy & Security · Page ID: security-alerts

The automatic security notifications we send and what to tell a client who received one they don't recognize.

Alerts we send

We automatically notify clients by email (and text if they opt in) when:

  • Their password is changed.
  • Their email address, phone number, or mailing address is changed (the notice goes to both the old and the new address).
  • A new bank account is linked.
  • 2FA settings change or a trusted device is added.
  • A new authorized user is added.
  • A sign-in occurs from a new device.
  • A withdrawal or outgoing transfer is requested.

A client didn't make the change

If a client receives an alert for something they did not do, treat it as possible account takeover. Follow Suspected account takeover or unauthorized activity: verify identity, secure the login, escalate to the Fraud team, and review recent activity.

A client wants fewer alerts

Security alerts for changes to credentials, contact details, banks, and access cannot be turned off. Marketing and informational emails can be managed at My Account → Preferences.

Example

Client: "I got an email saying my phone number was changed. I didn't do that."

Agent: "Thank you for telling us right away. Let's secure your account. First, I need to verify a few details..." Then follow Suspected account takeover or unauthorized activity: verify identity, escalate to Fraud, reset the password, review recent activity, and remove unknown devices.

Alerts and joint accounts

Changes made by one joint owner to the shared account (for example, adding a bank) generate alerts to both owners.
