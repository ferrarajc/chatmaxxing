Roth conversions: overview

Section: Roth Conversions · Page ID: roth-conversion-overview

What a Roth conversion is, who can do one, how it is taxed, and why it can't be undone.

What it is

A Roth conversion moves money from a Traditional IRA or SEP-IRA (or from a pre-tax employer plan) into a Roth IRA. The pre-tax amount converted is added to the client's taxable income for the year. Afterward, the money grows in the Roth IRA, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

Who can convert

Anyone with a Traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRA (after two years) can convert any amount. There is no income limit and no annual limit on conversions. Conversions do not count toward the contribution limit.

Taxes

  • The converted amount is taxable as ordinary income, except for any nondeductible basis (pro-rata across all of the client's Traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs). See How conversions are taxed.
  • The 10% early-withdrawal penalty does not apply to the conversion itself, as long as no tax is withheld from it. Withholding tax from a conversion makes the withheld amount a distribution, which can be penalized for clients under 59½.

Irreversible

Since 2018, conversions cannot be recharacterized (undone). If the market falls after a conversion, or the client's income turns out higher than expected, the conversion stands. Make sure the client understands this before submitting.

RMDs first

A client subject to RMDs must take the year's RMD before converting; RMD amounts cannot be converted.

The advice boundary

Whether to convert, and how much, is a tax planning decision. You may explain how conversions work. You may not recommend one. Clients considering conversions are good candidates for a callback with a licensed professional.
