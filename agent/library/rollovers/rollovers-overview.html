Rollovers and transfers: overview

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: rollovers-overview

The difference between rollovers and transfers, which to use when, and how each is taxed and reported.

Terms

TermWhat it isExample
Direct rolloverMoney moves from an employer plan straight to an IRA, payable to the new custodian401(k) at a former employer → Bob's Traditional IRA
Indirect (60-day) rolloverThe client receives the money and redeposits it within 60 daysA 401(k) check payable to the client, deposited at Bob's
Trustee-to-trustee transferMoney moves between two IRAs of the same type at different firms, never paid to the clientTraditional IRA at another firm → Bob's Traditional IRA
ACAT transferAn industry system for moving brokerage accounts (taxable or IRA) between firms, in cash or in kindTaxable brokerage account → Bob's individual account

Tax treatment

  • Direct rollovers and trustee-to-trustee transfers are not taxable and have no withholding.
  • Indirect rollovers are not taxable if completed within 60 days and the client makes up any withholding. See Indirect (60-day) rollovers.
  • None of these count toward the annual IRA contribution limit.

Matching account types

Pre-tax money goes to a Traditional IRA; Roth money goes to a Roth IRA. Rolling pre-tax money into a Roth IRA is a conversion and is taxable. See Rolling over a Roth 401(k) and Roth conversions: overview.

How clients start

  • From an employer plan: the client contacts the plan administrator and requests a direct rollover to Bob's Mutual Funds. We help with our receiving details. In chat, use Procedure: Roll Over From Another Institution (chat).
  • From another IRA or brokerage account: the client starts the transfer with us (My Account → Transfer Assets), and we request the assets from the other firm. See Incoming ACAT transfers.

Timelines

Direct rollovers: 5–15 business days after the plan processes the request. ACAT transfers: 5–7 business days. Non-ACAT transfers: 2–4 weeks.
