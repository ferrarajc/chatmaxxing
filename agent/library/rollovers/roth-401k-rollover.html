Rolling over a Roth 401(k)

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: roth-401k-rollover

Moving Roth 401(k) money into a Roth IRA, the five-year clock, and mixed accounts.

On this page

  • Where it goes
  • The five-year clock
  • Mixed accounts
  • RMDs
  • Example
  • Employer match

Where it goes

Roth 401(k) (designated Roth) money must go to a Roth IRA to remain Roth. It can never go to a Traditional IRA.

The five-year clock

The Roth 401(k)'s participation period does not carry over to the Roth IRA. Whether earnings are qualified in the Roth IRA depends on the Roth IRA five-year clock, which starts with the client's first Roth IRA contribution or conversion. A client who has never had a Roth IRA starts a new clock with the rollover. See Roth five-year rules.

Mixed accounts

Most plans with Roth money also hold pre-tax money (including employer matches, which were historically pre-tax). The plan usually sends two checks: one for the Roth portion (to the Roth IRA) and one for the pre-tax portion (to the Traditional IRA). Make sure both receiving accounts exist and the client gives the plan both account numbers.

RMDs

Roth 401(k)s no longer require RMDs for the owner (since 2024), and Roth IRAs never do.

Example

A client's former employer plan holds $60,000 pre-tax and $25,000 Roth. The plan sends two checks: $60,000 to the client's Traditional IRA and $25,000 to their Roth IRA. If the client has no Roth IRA, open one first. See Procedure: Open a New Account (chat).

Employer match

Employer matching contributions were traditionally pre-tax, even when the employee contributed to the Roth 401(k). Newer plans may allow Roth matching. The plan's statement shows which is which.
