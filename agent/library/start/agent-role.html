The chat agent's role

Section: Start Here · Page ID: agent-role

What chat agents at Bob's Mutual Funds do, what they do not do, and how responsibility is shared between the agent and the AI tools.

  • Handling multiple chats

What you are here to do

As a chat agent you are the client's human point of contact for anything the self-service portal and the virtual assistant could not resolve. Clients reach you after the virtual assistant has escalated their chat, when they ask for a person, or when they continue a recent conversation with you. Your job is to understand what the client is trying to accomplish, get it done accurately, and leave the client confident about what happens next.

In practice that means:

  • Answering questions about accounts, funds, rules, deadlines, forms, and processes, accurately and in plain language.
  • Completing service tasks on the client's behalf: purchases, sales, exchanges, beneficiary changes, automatic investment schedules, distributions, RMD settings, rollovers, Roth conversions, tax document requests, contact updates, security changes, and callbacks. The full list is in Task experts and the task menu.
  • Protecting the client by verifying identity, watching for fraud and exploitation, and refusing requests that would harm them or violate policy.
  • Routing the client to the right specialist when a request is outside chat scope (complex estates, licensed advice, certain trades by phone).
  • Documenting the interaction through after-chat work so the next person who helps the client knows what happened.

What you do not do

Chat agents are not licensed to give individualized investment, tax, or legal advice. You can explain how products and rules work; you cannot tell a client what they should do with their money. This boundary is explained in detail in The investment advice boundary, and it is the single most important compliance rule in this job.

You also do not:

  • Promise outcomes you do not control (exact settlement dates for another institution's transfer, IRS treatment of a specific return, future fund performance).
  • Bypass verification because a client is in a hurry or upset.
  • Make changes the client has not clearly confirmed, even if the AI support panel suggests them.
  • Share one client's information with another person, including spouses and family members, unless that person is an authorized user with the right access level. See Authorized users and access levels.

Working with the AI tools

The agent workspace includes AI assistance: a suggested reply for every turn, predicted topics and questions, relevant client-safe resources, an autopilot that can carry routine parts of a conversation, and task experts that collect the details a service task needs and present them as a Proposed Action for your approval. These tools make you faster. They do not replace your judgment.

The rule is simple: you are accountable for everything that is sent to the client and everything that is submitted on the client's behalf. Before you send a suggested reply, read it. Before you approve a Proposed Action, check every field against the transcript (the evidence highlighting helps; see Evidence highlighting). If autopilot is running and something looks wrong, pause it. See Autopilot controls: pause, resume, exit, send now.

Handling multiple chats

You may handle up to four chats at once, one per column. Concurrency is a privilege earned by accuracy. If a conversation becomes complex or emotional (a bereavement, a fraud report, a complaint), focus on it. You can let other columns run on autopilot for routine collection or ask your team lead to stop routing new contacts to you. See Service standards and response times for response-time expectations.

Your professional standard

Clients trust us with their retirement savings. Every chat should leave the client feeling that someone competent and careful handled their request. That means being accurate before being fast, being plain rather than clever, and being honest about what you do not know.
