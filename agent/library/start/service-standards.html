Service standards and response times

Section: Start Here · Page ID: service-standards

The response-time, accuracy, and quality expectations for every chat, and how they are measured.

Why we have standards

Clients judge us by how quickly we respond and whether we get it right the first time. Standards give every agent the same target and give team leads a fair basis for coaching. They are not a reason to rush: an accurate answer delivered thirty seconds later is always better than a fast wrong one.

Response-time standards

MomentStandard
Accept an incoming contactBefore the alert countdown expires
First message after acceptingWithin 30 seconds. The intent summary and suggested greeting are ready when you accept; use them
Reply to each client messageWithin 60 seconds on average; never more than 2 minutes without a message
Research that takes longerTell the client you are checking and give a time estimate (see Holds and delays)
Idle clientThe system sends a check-in after a period of silence; after a second unanswered check-in you may close the chat with a closing message

The response timer on each column shows how long the client has been waiting. When it turns amber, send something, even if it is only "I'm still checking that for you."

Accuracy standards

  • Every factual statement is supportable from this library or from the client's account data. If you cannot point to the source, do not state it as fact.
  • Every Proposed Action is checked field by field against what the client said before you approve it. Accuracy errors on submitted actions are the most serious quality defect.
  • Every confirmation recap is explicit. Before submitting a task, recap the account, the fund, the amount, and any timing, and get the client's clear "yes". See Confirmation recaps.

Quality standards

Quality reviewers and the AI coaching in after-chat work evaluate chats on:

  1. Understanding: did you identify what the client actually needed, including anything unstated?
  2. Accuracy: was every answer correct and complete?
  3. Compliance: was identity verified, was advice avoided, were disclosures given?
  4. Clarity: plain language, no jargon without explanation, short paragraphs.
  5. Ownership: did you finish the job or hand off cleanly with a clear next step?
  6. Warmth: did the client feel listened to?

First-contact resolution

Aim to resolve the client's request in the chat they started. When that is not possible (a specialist team must act, documents are required, an outside institution is involved), the chat still counts as well handled if the client leaves knowing exactly what will happen, when, and what they need to do. Schedule a callback rather than asking the client to "call back later". See Scheduling callbacks.

Concurrency

You may hold up to four chats. Your team lead may lower your limit during training or after quality issues. If you feel overloaded, tell your team lead rather than letting response times slip across every column.
