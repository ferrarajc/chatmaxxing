The phase-out ranges (modified adjusted gross income)

Filing status2026 full contribution if MAGI belowPartial contributionNo direct contribution if MAGI at or above
Single, head of household$153,000$153,000–$168,000$168,000
Married filing jointly$242,000$242,000–$252,000$252,000
Married filing separately (lived with spouse)—$0–$10,000$10,000

2025 figures: single $150,000–$165,000; married filing jointly $236,000–$246,000.

Partial contributions

Within the range, the allowed contribution is reduced proportionally. For example, a single filer with 2026 MAGI of $160,500 is halfway through the $15,000 range, so they can contribute about half the limit ($3,750 if under 50). The IRS worksheet rounds to the nearest $10. Suggest the client confirm the exact figure with their tax preparer.

Our role

We do not collect or verify income. The client certifies their eligibility. If a client tells you their income and it is above the limit, tell them before processing a Roth contribution. If they contribute anyway and are ineligible, it becomes an excess contribution that must be corrected. See Excess contributions and how to fix them.

When clients exceed the limit

Options include contributing to a Traditional IRA instead (anyone with earned income can, though the deduction may be limited) or a recharacterization. Some clients use a "backdoor Roth"; see The "backdoor Roth" strategy for how to discuss it. Never recommend a strategy.

MAGI

Modified adjusted gross income for Roth purposes is generally the client's adjusted gross income with certain items added back. The client's tax preparer can calculate it; we cannot.
