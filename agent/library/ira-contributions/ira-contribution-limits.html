IRA contribution limits

Section: IRA Contributions · Page ID: ira-contribution-limits

The 2026 and 2025 Traditional and Roth IRA limits, how the shared limit works, and how to check a client's remaining room.

Current limits

Tax yearUnder 50Age 50 or older (with catch-up)Contribution deadline
2026$7,500$8,600 ($7,500 + $1,100)April 15, 2027
2025$7,000$8,000 ($7,000 + $1,000)April 15, 2026 (passed)

A client is eligible for the catch-up for a tax year if they turn 50 at any time during that year, even on December 31.

One limit, shared

The limit applies to the total of the client's Traditional and Roth IRA contributions for the year, across every IRA they own at every firm. It is not per account. A client who contributes $4,000 to a Roth at Bob's and $3,500 to a Traditional IRA elsewhere has used the full $7,500 for 2026.

Other constraints

  • Contributions cannot exceed the client's taxable compensation for the year (or the couple's combined compensation for a spousal IRA). See The earned income (compensation) rule.
  • Roth contributions phase out at higher incomes. See Roth IRA income limits.
  • SEP-IRA employer contributions have a separate, much higher limit. See SEP-IRA contributions.
  • Rollovers, transfers, and conversions do not count toward the limit.

Checking remaining room

The contributions summary in the client info panel and on IRA account pages shows contributions made at Bob's for the tax year, against the client's limit based on their age. Remaining room = limit − contributions shown − contributions the client made elsewhere. We cannot see contributions at other firms; ask the client if it matters. See The IRA contributions summary.

Common questions

"How much more can I put in this year?"

"You've contributed $3,000 to IRAs with us for 2026, and your limit is $7,500, so you can contribute up to $4,500 more, as long as you haven't made IRA contributions anywhere else this year."

"Is the limit per account?"

No. One limit across all Traditional and Roth IRAs.

"My spouse and I both have IRAs."

Each spouse has their own limit.
