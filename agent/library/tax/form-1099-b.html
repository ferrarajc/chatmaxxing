Form 1099-B

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: form-1099-b

How sales and exchanges in taxable accounts are reported, including cost basis, holding period, and covered status.

On this page

  • What it reports
  • Covered and noncovered
  • Sections of the form
  • Cost basis method
  • Exchanges
  • Common questions

What it reports

Every sale and exchange in a taxable account during the year: the date sold, proceeds, cost basis (for covered shares), date acquired, whether the gain or loss is short-term or long-term, and any wash-sale adjustments.

Covered and noncovered

  • Covered shares (fund shares acquired since January 1, 2012): we report cost basis to the IRS.
  • Noncovered shares (acquired before 2012, or transferred in without basis): we may show basis if we have it, but do not report it to the IRS; the client is responsible.

See Covered and noncovered shares.

Sections of the form

The 1099-B is organized by short-term and long-term, and covered and noncovered, matching the categories the client reports on IRS Form 8949.

Cost basis method

The basis shown depends on the method in effect when the shares were sold: average cost (default), FIFO, or specific identification. See Cost basis methods.

Exchanges

An exchange appears as a sale of the "from" fund. Clients often forget this.

Common questions

"The basis is missing."

The shares may be noncovered. The client can use their own records, or contact us for any historical information we have (Tax Reporting).

"The gain looks too high."

Check whether reinvested dividends are included in the basis (they are, for covered shares) and whether the method is what the client expected. Refer suspected errors to Tax Reporting.
