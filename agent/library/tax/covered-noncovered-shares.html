Covered and noncovered shares

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: covered-noncovered-shares

Which shares have cost basis reported to the IRS, why, and what clients need for older shares.

On this page

  • Definitions
  • Transfers in
  • Inherited and gifted shares
  • Finding old basis
  • Example
  • Average cost and the 2012 split

Definitions

  • Covered: mutual fund shares acquired on or after January 1, 2012. We track and report their basis to the IRS on Form 1099-B.
  • Noncovered: shares acquired before 2012, or transferred from another firm without basis information. Basis is the client's responsibility.

Transfers in

When shares transfer to us from another firm (where the shares are BobsFunds shares), the delivering firm should send basis information for covered shares within 15 days. If it doesn't arrive, the shares are treated as noncovered until updated.

Inherited and gifted shares

Inherited shares receive date-of-death basis, which we record when Estate Services processes the transfer. Gifted shares carry the giver's basis, which the giver should provide.

Finding old basis

For noncovered shares, clients can use old statements and confirmations. We may have historical records; requests go to Tax Reporting.

Example

A client has owned BF500 since 2008 and kept buying through 2026. Shares bought before 2012 are noncovered; shares bought since are covered. When they sell, the 1099-B shows covered and noncovered sales in separate sections. For noncovered shares, we may show basis "for information only", which the client should verify.

Average cost and the 2012 split

Under average cost, covered and noncovered shares are averaged separately. Clients who switch methods should ask their tax advisor about the effect.
