The wash-sale rule

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: wash-sales

What a wash sale is, how it can affect clients who sell at a loss, and what we report.

The rule

If a client sells shares at a loss and buys the same or a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss is disallowed (deferred). The disallowed loss is added to the basis of the replacement shares.

Common triggers

  • Selling a fund at a loss while an automatic investment buys more of the same fund within 30 days.
  • Dividend reinvestments within 30 days of the loss sale.
  • Buying the same fund in an IRA within 30 days (the loss is permanently disallowed in that case).

What we report

On Form 1099-B, we report wash sales we can identify within the same account for the same fund. We do not track purchases at other firms or in other accounts; the client is responsible for those.

"Substantially identical"

Whether two different funds (for example, two different S&P 500 index funds) are substantially identical is not clearly defined. It is a question for the client's tax advisor.

In chat

Explain the rule if asked, and note that automatic investments and dividend reinvestment can trigger it. Do not advise how to structure sales.

Example

A client sells 100 shares of BF500 at a $1,000 loss on November 10. Their automatic investment buys 2 shares of BF500 on November 15. The loss on 2 of the 100 shares (about $20) is disallowed and added to the basis of the 2 new shares; the remaining $980 loss is allowed.

Reinvested dividends

A December dividend reinvestment within 30 days of a loss sale can create a small wash sale. Clients who want to avoid this sometimes turn off reinvestment temporarily; that is the client's choice.

Reporting

Form 1099-B shows "wash sale loss disallowed" amounts for wash sales we identify. The adjusted basis carries to the replacement shares.
