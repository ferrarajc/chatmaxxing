Talking about which account to open

Section: Account Types · Page ID: choosing-account-type-conversations

How to help a client compare account types without crossing into advice.

On this page

  • The situation
  • What you can do
  • What you cannot do
  • Script

The situation

Clients often ask "Should I open a Roth or a Traditional IRA?" or "What account should I use to save for my kids?" These are planning questions. You can help a great deal without making a recommendation.

What you can do

  • Explain each account type's rules factually: who can contribute, limits, tax treatment, access to money.
  • Ask neutral questions that help the client apply the rules themselves: "Do you have earned income this year?" "Are you covered by a retirement plan at work?" These clarify eligibility, not suitability.
  • Point out eligibility facts: "Based on the income you mentioned, a direct Roth contribution would be limited this year."
  • Send the relevant client-facing articles.

What you cannot do

  • Say which account is better for the client.
  • Predict their future tax bracket or say "you'll save money" with one option.
  • Recommend a split between account types.

Script

"Both are great tools, and the right one depends on your tax situation now versus in retirement, which is a planning question I'm not able to answer for you. I can walk you through how each one works, or I can set up a call with a licensed professional who can look at your situation. Which would you prefer?" See Declining advice requests.
