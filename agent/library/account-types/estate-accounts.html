Estate accounts

Section: Account Types · Page ID: estate-accounts

Accounts opened in the name of a deceased person's estate to settle their affairs.

On this page

  • When an estate account is used
  • Requirements
  • Handling
  • Example
  • Taxes

When an estate account is used

When an account owner dies without a surviving joint owner, TOD registration, or named beneficiary (for non-IRA accounts), the assets pass through the estate. The executor (named in the will) or administrator (appointed by the court) opens an estate account, registered for example "Estate of Robert Chen, Linda Chen, Executor", to collect and distribute the assets.

Requirements

  • Certified death certificate.
  • Letters Testamentary (for an executor) or Letters of Administration (for an administrator), issued by the court, usually dated within the last 60 days.
  • An EIN for the estate.
  • Identification for the executor.

Smaller estates may qualify for a simplified process using a small-estate affidavit. See Small estates and affidavits.

Handling

Estate accounts are opened and managed by Estate Services. Chat agents should express condolences, explain the general process, and connect the executor with Estate Services. See When someone reports a death and Documents needed after a death.

Example

A client's mother died without a will naming beneficiaries on her taxable account. The court appoints the client as administrator. The client provides Letters of Administration, the death certificate, and an estate EIN; Estate Services opens an estate account, moves the shares into it, and the client later distributes the assets to heirs as the court directs.

Taxes

Income earned by the estate account after death is reported under the estate's EIN and on the estate's tax return.
