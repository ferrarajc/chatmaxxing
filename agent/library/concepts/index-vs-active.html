Index funds and active funds

Section: Investing Concepts (Educational) · Page ID: index-vs-active

How index funds differ from actively managed funds, and why our lineup is mostly index funds.

Definitions

  • Index fund: tracks a published index (for example, the S&P 500) by holding the same securities in the same proportions. Low turnover, low cost.
  • Actively managed fund: a manager picks securities trying to beat a benchmark. Higher costs and turnover.

The evidence clients may have heard about

Research over many decades (for example, the S&P SPIVA reports) has found that most actively managed funds trail their benchmarks over long periods, after costs. This is a general finding, not a guarantee about any fund.

Our lineup

Nearly all BobsFunds funds are index funds, reflecting our founder's philosophy that low costs and broad diversification are the most reliable drivers of long-term results. The client Library has a guide, "Index Funds vs. Active Funds".

How to discuss

Explain the difference. You may describe our philosophy as the firm's view. Do not say index funds are right for the client.

Our expense ratios as context

Our index funds cost 0.03%–0.13% a year. See Expense ratios.

Common client question

"Can an index fund beat the market?"

By design an index fund aims to match its index, minus small costs. It will not beat its index by much, and it won't trail by much either.
