Communicating with grieving clients

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: bereavement-communication

How to write to someone who has lost a loved one: tone, pacing, words to use and avoid.

On this page

  • Principles
  • Words that help
  • Words to avoid
  • When the caller is also a client
  • Look after yourself

Principles

  • Acknowledge first. Before anything procedural: "I'm so sorry for the loss of your mother."
  • Slow down. Shorter messages, fewer questions at a time, and no autopilot.
  • Use plain words. Say "died" or "passed away" (mirror the client's words). Avoid euphemisms that confuse and jargon like "decedent" or "beneficial owner".
  • Explain the next step, not every step. A grieving person can absorb one or two actions. Put the rest in writing (a secure message or the checklist from Estate Services).
  • Let them set the pace. If they need to stop, offer a callback.

Words that help

  • "Take whatever time you need."
  • "You don't need to decide anything today."
  • "I'll make sure Estate Services has everything you've told me, so you won't have to repeat it."
  • "Is there anything else I can help with right now?"

Words to avoid

  • "I know how you feel."
  • "At least they lived a long life."
  • "Per our policy..."
  • Anything about investment performance or "what to do with the money".

When the caller is also a client

If a grieving client asks about their own accounts (for example, a surviving spouse), help them as usual, gently. They may have urgent needs (paying bills); answer clearly and connect them with Estate Services for the deceased's accounts.

Look after yourself

These conversations can be hard. Take a moment after the chat if you need it, and talk with your team lead.
