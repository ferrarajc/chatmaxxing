Documents needed after a death

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: estate-documentation

The documents Estate Services typically requires, by account type and claimant.

On this page

  • Always
  • By situation
  • How to submit
  • Timeline

Always

  • A certified copy of the death certificate (a photocopy is not enough; certified copies are available from the county or state vital records office or the funeral home).
  • Government-issued photo ID for each person claiming assets.

By situation

SituationAdditional documents
Beneficiary of an IRABeneficiary claim form (provided by Estate Services); inherited IRA application or distribution election
TOD beneficiary of a taxable accountBeneficiary claim form; new account application
Surviving joint owner (JTWROS)Affidavit of domicile (some cases); re-registration form
Executor or administrator (estate account)Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration, certified, generally dated within 60 days; estate EIN; estate account application
Small estate (no probate)Small-estate affidavit as permitted by state law. See Small estates and affidavits
Trust owned the accountCertification of trust showing the successor trustee
Medallion signature guaranteeFor some transfers of assets to beneficiaries or between registrations

How to submit

Upload through a secure message or the document upload page, or mail certified copies to the PO Box (or overnight to the street address). We do not need originals. Estate Services sends a personalized checklist within 1–2 business days of first contact.

Timeline

Once documentation is complete, transfers typically finish in 5–10 business days. Complex estates or disputes take longer. See Estate settlement timelines.
