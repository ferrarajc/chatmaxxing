Expense ratios

Section: Fund Lineup · Page ID: expense-ratios

What an expense ratio is, how it is charged, the range across our lineup, and how to explain cost to clients.

On this page

  • Definition
  • How it is charged
  • Our range
  • Common questions

Definition

The expense ratio is the annual cost of operating a fund, expressed as a percentage of the fund's assets. It covers management, administration, custody, and other fund costs. A 0.03% expense ratio means $3 per year for every $10,000 invested.

How it is charged

The expense ratio is not billed to the client's account. It is accrued daily inside the fund and reflected in the NAV. The returns we publish are already net of expenses. This is why a client never sees a line item for it on a statement.

Our range

BobsFunds expense ratios range from 0.03% (BF500, BFTM, BFBI) to 0.13% (BFREI). Sector funds are 0.09%. See The BobsFunds lineup for every fund.

Common questions

"How much am I paying in fees?"

Multiply each holding's value by its expense ratio. A client with $40,000 in BF500 pays about $12 a year. There are no other account fees unless they use a paid service such as an outgoing wire. See Fee schedule.

"Why did the expense ratio change?"

Expense ratios are updated in each annual prospectus and can go up or down slightly as fund assets and costs change. The current prospectus figure applies.

"An old page says BFGR costs 0.25%."

Some older client materials listed higher expense ratios for the original funds. The current prospectus figure (shown on the fund's profile and Research pages) is authoritative. Apologize for any confusion and report the outdated page.
