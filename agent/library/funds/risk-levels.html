Published risk levels

Section: Fund Lineup · Page ID: risk-levels

The five risk levels shown on fund pages, what they are based on, and how to discuss them without advising.

On this page

  • The scale
  • What it is not
  • How to discuss it
  • Standard deviation and beta

The scale

Every fund page shows one of five risk levels: Low, Low–Medium, Medium, Medium–High, High. The level reflects the fund's historical volatility and the nature of its holdings.

LevelTypical fundsGeneral characteristics
LowBFSTShort-term government bonds; small price movements
Low–MediumBFBI, BFITT, BFSTC, BFSTP, BFUTLIntermediate bonds or defensive stock sectors
MediumBF500, BFTM, BFIN, BFDEV, BFLTTBroad diversified stock funds; long Treasuries
Medium–HighBFGR, BFMC, sector funds such as BFFIN, BFLTCMore concentrated or more rate-sensitive
HighBFSC, BFSCV, BFSCG, BFMCG, BFTEC, BFENE, BFEMG, BFDISSmall companies, single sectors, emerging markets

What it is not

The risk level describes the fund, not whether it suits the client. Every fund, including the Low-risk fund, can lose value. A risk level is not a prediction.

How to discuss it

Quote the risk level and explain what it means in general terms. If the client asks "is this too risky for me?", that is a suitability question. Say: "I can tell you how this fund is rated and how it has behaved historically; whether it fits your situation is something a licensed professional can help you decide." See Declining advice requests.

Standard deviation and beta

Fund profiles also show standard deviation (how much returns have varied, annualized over three years) and beta (how much the fund has moved relative to the S&P 500; 1.0 means in line, above 1 means more, below 1 means less). These are historical statistics.
