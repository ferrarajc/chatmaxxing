Comparing funds with a client

Section: Fund Lineup · Page ID: fund-comparison-guide

How to compare funds side by side using published facts, without recommending one.

On this page

  • Facts you can compare
  • How to present a comparison
  • What not to say
  • When the client asks you to choose

Facts you can compare

When a client asks you to compare funds, use only published characteristics:

CharacteristicWhere to find it
Objective and holdingsFund profile "What the fund does"
Category and groupFund profile, lineup table
Expense ratioFund profile
Risk level, standard deviation, betaFund profile
Calendar-year returnsFund profile; trailing returns on the Research page
Yield and distribution frequencyFund profile
Duration (bond funds)Fund profile
Number of holdings, sector allocationFund profile

How to present a comparison

Present the facts in parallel and let the client draw conclusions:

"Here's how they compare. BF500 holds about 500 large U.S. companies, costs 0.03%, and is rated Medium risk. BFGR holds about 235 large growth companies, costs 0.04%, and is rated Medium–High; it has had bigger swings in both directions, for example +46.8% in 2023 and −33.2% in 2022, versus +26.3% and −18.2% for BF500."

What not to say

  • "This one is better."
  • "I'd go with..." or "Most people pick..."
  • "Growth funds always beat the market over time."
  • Any forecast of future returns.

When the client asks you to choose

Offer a callback with a licensed professional. Use the Fund Comparison wrap-up code if the chat was mainly a comparison, or Investment Advice if the client was referred. See Declining advice requests.
