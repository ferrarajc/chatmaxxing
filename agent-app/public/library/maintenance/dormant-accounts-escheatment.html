Dormant accounts and unclaimed property

Section: Account Maintenance · Page ID: dormant-accounts-escheatment

When accounts become dormant, how state unclaimed property laws apply, and how clients reclaim escheated assets.

On this page

  • Dormancy
  • Escheatment
  • Preventing it
  • Reclaiming
  • Retirement accounts

Dormancy

An account is considered dormant when the owner has had no contact with us for a period set by state law, usually three to five years. Contact includes signing in, placing a transaction, calling, or responding to mail. Automatic investments and dividend reinvestments generally do not count as owner contact.

Escheatment

Each state requires financial firms to turn over (escheat) dormant property to the state's unclaimed property program. Before escheating, we mail a due-diligence letter to the last known address. If the owner does not respond, the shares are liquidated or transferred to the state.

Preventing it

Clients prevent dormancy simply by signing in at least once a year or by keeping their address current. Returned mail accelerates the process. See Address changes: special situations.

Reclaiming

A client whose assets were escheated can reclaim them from the state's unclaimed property office (searchable at missingmoney.com). If the shares were liquidated, the state returns the cash value at the time of liquidation, not current value. We can provide documentation of the escheatment through Account Services.

Retirement accounts

IRAs are escheated less often because of their long-term nature, but the same contact rules apply in most states.
