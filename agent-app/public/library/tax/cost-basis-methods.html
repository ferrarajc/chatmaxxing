Cost basis methods

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: cost-basis-methods

The three methods available (average cost, FIFO, specific identification), how to change, and how each works.

On this page

  • What cost basis is
  • Methods
  • Changing the method
  • Example

What cost basis is

Cost basis is what the client paid for their shares, including reinvested dividends and capital gains. When they sell, the gain or loss is proceeds minus basis.

Methods

MethodHow it worksNotes
Average cost (default)The basis per share is the average of all shares ownedSimple; most common for mutual funds
FIFO (first in, first out)The oldest shares are sold firstOldest shares often have the lowest basis (larger gains) but are long-term
Specific identificationThe client chooses which lots to sellRequires choosing lots at the time of each sale

Changing the method

Clients change the method at My Account → Tax Settings, per fund or for the account. The method must be in place before a sale; it cannot be changed retroactively for shares already sold. Changing away from average cost applies to future sales; the basis of shares already averaged may remain averaged.

Example

A client bought 10 shares at $100 and 10 at $150 and sells 10 at $160.

  • Average cost: basis $125/share; gain $350.
  • FIFO: basis $100/share; gain $600.
  • Specific ID (choosing the $150 shares): gain $100.

What you can say

Explain the methods and how to change them. Which method to use is a tax decision.
