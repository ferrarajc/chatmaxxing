Form W-9 and backup withholding

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: w9-backup-withholding

Taxpayer ID certification, TIN mismatches, and why backup withholding happens.

Form W-9

Every U.S. client certifies their name and taxpayer identification number (SSN or EIN) on a Form W-9 at account opening. This certification also states they are not subject to backup withholding.

Backup withholding

We must withhold 24% of dividends and sale proceeds in a taxable account (backup withholding) if:

  • The client has not provided a certified TIN.
  • The IRS notifies us that the name and TIN don't match (a "B notice").
  • The IRS notifies us that the client is subject to backup withholding for under-reporting.

Backup withholding is reported on Form 1099 and is credited against the client's tax.

Fixing a TIN mismatch

The client must confirm their name and SSN match Social Security records (often a name change not reported to SSA) and submit a new Form W-9, or in some cases a copy of their Social Security card. Account Services processes it. See Legal name changes.

Non-U.S. persons

Non-resident clients provide Form W-8BEN instead. See Non-U.S. persons and clients living abroad.

B notices

When the IRS tells us a client's name and TIN don't match, we send the client a "B notice" letter with instructions. If the client doesn't fix it in time, backup withholding begins. A second B notice within three years requires the client to validate their TIN with the IRS or SSA.

Stopping backup withholding

Once the client provides a corrected, certified TIN (and any required validation), backup withholding stops for future payments. Amounts already withheld are credited on the client's tax return.
