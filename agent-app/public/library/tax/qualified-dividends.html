Qualified dividends

Section: Tax Center · Page ID: qualified-dividends

What makes a dividend qualified, which of our funds pay them, and the holding period requirement.

On this page

  • Definition
  • Which funds
  • Holding period
  • Examples
  • Where the client sees it

Definition

Qualified dividends are taxed at long-term capital gain rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) instead of ordinary income rates. Most dividends from U.S. companies and many foreign companies qualify.

Which funds

  • U.S. stock funds (BF500, BFTM, BFGR, and others): mostly qualified.
  • International funds: largely qualified (dividends from companies in treaty countries).
  • REIT fund (BFREI): REIT dividends are mostly not qualified (but may qualify for the Section 199A deduction).
  • Bond funds: interest is not qualified.

The fund reports the qualified portion in box 1b of Form 1099-DIV.

Holding period

The client must hold the fund shares for more than 60 days during the 121-day period around the ex-dividend date for dividends to be treated as qualified. Most long-term holders meet this; very short-term holders might not.

Examples

FundTypical treatment of its dividends
BF500, BFTM, BFDGA, BFHDYMostly qualified
BFIN, BFDEV, BFEUR, BFPACLargely qualified
BFEMGPartly qualified (depends on countries)
BFREIMostly not qualified; may be Section 199A dividends
BFBI, BFST, and other bond fundsNot qualified (interest income)

Where the client sees it

Box 1a of Form 1099-DIV shows total ordinary dividends; box 1b shows the qualified portion included in 1a.
