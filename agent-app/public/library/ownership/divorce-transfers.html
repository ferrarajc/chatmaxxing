Divorce and account transfers

How assets are divided in a divorce: IRAs, taxable accounts, joint accounts, and what documents we need.

IRAs: transfer incident to divorce

An IRA can be divided between spouses without tax only under a divorce decree or separation agreement. The decree must direct the transfer. We then transfer the specified amount (or percentage) from one spouse's IRA directly into an IRA in the other spouse's name. Done this way, it is not a distribution and not taxable to either spouse. Afterward, the receiving spouse owns that IRA.

A client who withdraws money from their IRA and gives it to their ex-spouse has made a taxable distribution, not a divorce transfer.

QDROs are for employer plans

A Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO) divides an employer plan such as a 401(k). IRAs do not need a QDRO; a decree suffices. Clients often use the terms interchangeably. See QDRO versus divorce decree.

Taxable accounts

Individual taxable accounts are transferred per the decree, in kind, to the receiving spouse's account. Transfers between spouses incident to divorce are not taxable; the cost basis carries over.

Joint accounts

Removing a former spouse from a joint account requires both owners' signatures, or the decree directing the change. Usually the assets are moved into individual accounts for each.

Documents

  • A certified copy of the divorce decree (or the relevant pages, plus the signature and judge's pages).
  • Our Divorce Transfer form, signed by the account owner (or the decree must be specific enough to act on without it).
  • An account at Bob's in the receiving spouse's name (we can help them open one).

Beneficiaries

After a divorce, clients often need to update beneficiaries. In some states, divorce automatically revokes an ex-spouse designation for certain assets, but clients should not rely on that. Suggest a review. See Reviewing beneficiaries after life events.

In chat

Explain the process, send the form, and route to Account Services. Be neutral and discreet; do not discuss one spouse's accounts with the other.
