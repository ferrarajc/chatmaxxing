What ACH is

ACH (Automated Clearing House) is the U.S. network for electronic bank transfers. It is free, reliable, and slower than a wire. We use it for purchases from a linked bank, automatic investments, redemptions to a bank, distributions by direct deposit, and cash dividends to the bank.

Timing

  • Deposits (debits from the client's bank): the purchase is priced on the trade date; the bank is debited within 2–3 business days.
  • Withdrawals (credits to the client's bank): sent the business day after the sale settles; arrives in 1–2 business days after that, typically 2–3 business days from the trade.

Some banks post incoming ACH credits early in the morning; others later in the day. We cannot control the bank's posting time.

Limits

  • $100,000 per day per account for deposits.
  • Withdrawals by ACH have no daily limit beyond the account's available cash, but very large withdrawals should go by wire.

Failures

ACH debits can be returned by the client's bank (insufficient funds, account closed, debit blocked). See Returned payments and items. ACH credits can be returned if the bank account is closed; the money comes back to the client's Bob's account as cash.

Business days

ACH does not move on weekends or bank holidays, including Columbus Day and Veterans Day when the stock market is open. See Holiday calendar 2026.

Explaining the timing to clients

"When you buy with your bank, you get today's price right away, and the money comes out of your bank in two to three business days. When you sell and send money to your bank, it usually arrives two to three business days after the sale."
