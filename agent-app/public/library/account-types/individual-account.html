Individual (taxable) accounts

Section: Account Types · Page ID: individual-account

The basic brokerage account owned by one person: features, taxes, and common questions.

What it is

An individual account is a taxable account owned by one person. It is the most flexible account we offer: no contribution limits, no income limits, no age restrictions on withdrawals, and no required distributions. The portal labels it "Taxable" or "Individual Taxable".

Taxes

Because the account is taxable, the owner pays tax each year on:

  • Dividends paid by the funds, whether received in cash or reinvested. Many are "qualified dividends" taxed at lower capital-gains rates. See Qualified dividends.
  • Capital gain distributions paid by the funds.
  • Capital gains or losses when the owner sells or exchanges shares. See Cost basis methods.

We report this income on the consolidated Form 1099 each year. See Form 1099-DIV and Form 1099-B.

Transfer on death

An individual account can carry a transfer-on-death (TOD) registration naming beneficiaries, so the assets pass directly to them without probate. See Transfer on death (TOD) for taxable accounts.

Common questions

Can I take money out any time?

Yes. Sell shares and have the proceeds sent to the linked bank by ACH, by wire, or by check. Selling may create a taxable gain or loss.

Is there a limit on how much I can put in?

No. Daily ACH deposits are limited to $100,000 per account; larger deposits can be made by wire. See Transfer limits.

Can I convert it to an IRA?

No. An individual account cannot be converted into an IRA. The client can sell shares and use the cash to make an IRA contribution, subject to IRA limits, but the sale may be taxable.

Can I add my spouse?

Adding an owner is a registration change, effectively moving the assets to a new joint account. See Registration changes.
