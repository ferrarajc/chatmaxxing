Roth IRA

Section: Account Types · Page ID: roth-ira

How the Roth IRA works: after-tax contributions, income limits, tax-free qualified withdrawals, and no RMDs for the owner.

On this page

  • Overview
  • Contributions
  • Withdrawals
  • No RMDs
  • Common questions

Overview

A Roth IRA is funded with after-tax money. Qualified withdrawals, including all earnings, are completely tax-free. The original owner never has to take required minimum distributions.

Contributions

  • Requires earned income (or a working spouse for a spousal Roth).
  • 2026 limit: $7,500, plus $1,100 catch-up at 50+; shared with Traditional IRA contributions.
  • Eligibility to contribute directly phases out at higher incomes. See Roth IRA income limits.
  • No age limit for contributions.

Withdrawals

Roth withdrawals follow ordering rules: contributions come out first, then conversions, then earnings.

  • Contributions can be withdrawn at any time, for any reason, with no tax or penalty.
  • Earnings are tax-free and penalty-free in a qualified distribution: the client is 59½ or older (or disabled, or the distribution is to a beneficiary, or up to $10,000 lifetime for a first home purchase) and the first Roth IRA was opened at least five tax years ago.
  • Non-qualified withdrawals of earnings may be taxed and penalized.

See Roth IRA distribution rules and Roth five-year rules.

No RMDs

Roth IRA owners are never required to take RMDs. Beneficiaries who inherit a Roth IRA are subject to inherited IRA rules. See Inherited IRA.

Common questions

My income is too high. Can I still have a Roth?

They cannot contribute directly above the phase-out range, but they can convert Traditional IRA money to Roth regardless of income. Some clients use a "backdoor Roth"; see The "backdoor Roth" strategy for the factual explanation and the advice boundary.

When does my 5-year clock start?

On January 1 of the tax year of the first contribution to any Roth IRA. A 2025 contribution made in March 2026 starts the clock on January 1, 2025.
