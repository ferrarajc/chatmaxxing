Traditional IRA

Section: Account Types · Page ID: traditional-ira

How the Traditional IRA works: contributions, deductibility, tax-deferred growth, withdrawals, and RMDs.

Overview

A Traditional IRA is an individual retirement account where investments grow tax-deferred. Contributions may be tax-deductible depending on income and workplace plan coverage. Withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income. Most money rolled over from pre-tax 401(k) and 403(b) plans goes into a Traditional IRA.

Contributions

  • Anyone with earned income (or a spouse with earned income) can contribute; there is no upper age limit.
  • Annual limit for 2026: $7,500, plus a $1,100 catch-up at age 50 or older. The limit is shared with Roth IRA contributions. See IRA contribution limits.
  • Contributions for a tax year can be made until the tax-filing deadline of the next year (April 15). See Contribution deadlines.
  • Deductibility depends on income if the client or their spouse is covered by a workplace plan. See Traditional IRA deductibility.

Withdrawals

  • Withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income (except any nondeductible "basis", which the client tracks on IRS Form 8606).
  • Withdrawals before age 59½ incur a 10% additional tax unless an exception applies. See The 10% early-withdrawal penalty and Exceptions to the early-withdrawal penalty.
  • We withhold 10% federal tax by default unless the client elects otherwise. See Federal tax withholding on IRA distributions.

Required minimum distributions

Owners must begin RMDs at age 73 (born 1951–1959) or 75 (born 1960 or later). See RMDs: overview.

Converting to Roth

All or part of a Traditional IRA can be converted to a Roth IRA; the converted pre-tax amount is taxable income. See Roth conversions: overview.

Common questions

Is a Traditional IRA better than a Roth?

That is a planning question. Explain the difference (tax break now versus tax-free later) and refer to a licensed professional for a recommendation.

Can I have both a Traditional and a Roth IRA?

Yes. The annual contribution limit applies to the combined total.
