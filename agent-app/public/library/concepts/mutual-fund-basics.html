Mutual funds explained

Section: Investing Concepts (Educational) · Page ID: mutual-fund-basics

What a mutual fund is, how it works, and how to explain it to a new investor.

On this page

  • The idea
  • Benefits clients often ask about
  • How it differs from an ETF
  • Explaining it simply
  • What not to say

The idea

A mutual fund pools money from many investors to buy a portfolio of stocks, bonds, or both. Each investor owns shares of the fund, and the value of their shares rises and falls with the value of everything the fund owns.

Benefits clients often ask about

  • Diversification: one fund can hold hundreds or thousands of securities, so no single company's troubles dominate. See Diversification.
  • Professional management: for index funds, management means tracking an index precisely and cheaply.
  • Low minimums: our funds start at $1.
  • Daily liquidity: shares can be sold any business day at that day's NAV.
  • Automatic features: reinvested dividends and automatic investing.

How it differs from an ETF

ETFs are also pooled funds, but they trade on an exchange throughout the day like stocks. Mutual funds trade once a day at NAV. Many index mutual funds and ETFs track the same indexes. See NAV: how fund prices work.

Explaining it simply

"A mutual fund is like a basket that holds many investments. When you buy a share, you own a small piece of the whole basket. Our 500 Index fund, for example, holds about 500 of the largest U.S. companies."

What not to say

Avoid implying a fund can't lose money. Every fund can lose value.
