Talking with clients during market downturns

Section: Investing Concepts (Educational) · Page ID: market-downturn-conversations

How to support anxious clients when markets fall, what you can say, and what you must not say.

On this page

  • What clients feel
  • What you can do
  • What you must not do
  • If the client wants to sell everything
  • Wrap-up

What clients feel

Falling markets frighten people, especially those near or in retirement. Clients may want reassurance, an explanation, or to sell everything.

What you can do

  • Acknowledge the feeling: "It's completely understandable to be concerned when you see your balance drop."
  • Explain what happened, factually: "Stock funds across the market declined this week" or "Bond prices fell as interest rates rose."
  • Provide context from published history: markets have experienced many declines, including −18.2% for BF500 in 2022, and past declines have been followed by recoveries, though past performance does not guarantee future results.
  • Point to educational resources: the client Library's "Investing Through Market Volatility" guide and "Bob's View: The Illusion of Market Timing" column.
  • Process what the client decides, accurately and without commentary.
  • Offer a callback with a licensed professional for guidance.

What you must not do

  • Predict where markets are going ("It'll bounce back soon").
  • Tell the client to stay invested or to sell.
  • Minimize the loss ("It's only a paper loss").
  • Express your own fear or opinions.

If the client wants to sell everything

Confirm what they want, once, clearly: "Just to confirm, you'd like to sell all of your BF500 and BFGR in your Traditional IRA and hold the proceeds as cash in the account?" Then process it. You may mention, factually, that selling locks in the current value and that in a taxable account sales can have tax consequences. Do not try to talk them out of it.

Wrap-up

Use Market Question, or Investment Advice if the client was referred.
