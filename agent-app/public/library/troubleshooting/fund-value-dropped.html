"Why did my fund go down?"

Section: Troubleshooting · Page ID: fund-value-dropped

How to explain a decline in a fund's value factually, including distribution-date drops.

On this page

  • Steps
  • What to avoid
  • Example explanations
  • Point to history

Steps

  1. Check the date and size of the drop.
  2. Distribution date? If the fund paid a distribution that day, the NAV dropped by the amount paid. The client didn't lose that value. See Record, ex-dividend, reinvestment, and pay dates.
  3. Market movement? Explain what the fund holds and that its value follows those markets. Use the fund profile for context (group, risk level, past calendar-year ranges).
  4. Bond fund? Rising interest rates lower bond prices. See Bonds and interest rates.
  5. International fund? A stronger dollar lowers the value of foreign holdings.

What to avoid

Explanations based on news events you can't verify, predictions, and advice. If the client is anxious, see Talking with clients during market downturns.

Example explanations

Distribution day: "BF500's price dropped $1.64 on March 25 because the fund paid a dividend of $1.64 per share that day. Your dividend was reinvested, so you now own a few more shares. Your total value wasn't reduced by the payment."

Market day: "BF500 holds the 500 largest U.S. companies, so it moves with the U.S. stock market. Yesterday the broad market fell about 2%, and the fund fell by a similar amount."

Bond fund: "BFLTT holds long-term Treasury bonds, which are sensitive to interest rates. When rates rise, their prices fall. The fund's average duration is about 15.5 years, so a 1 percentage-point rise in rates would lower its price roughly 15%."

International fund: "BFEUR holds European stocks. Its value in dollars depends on both European stock prices and the euro's exchange rate."

Point to history

Each fund profile shows calendar-year returns, which helps clients see that declines have happened before. Pair any history with "past performance doesn't guarantee future results".
