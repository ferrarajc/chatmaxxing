Options for non-spouse beneficiaries

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: non-spouse-beneficiaries

The 10-year rule and other options for individuals who inherit an IRA and are not the owner's spouse.

On this page

  • The 10-year rule
  • Options
  • Multiple beneficiaries
  • Eligible designated beneficiaries
  • In chat

The 10-year rule

Most non-spouse individual beneficiaries of owners who died after 2019 are designated beneficiaries subject to the 10-year rule: the inherited IRA must be fully distributed by December 31 of the 10th year after the year of death. If the owner had started RMDs, the beneficiary also takes annual RMDs in years 1–9. See Inherited IRA RMDs.

Options

  • Inherited IRA: transfer the beneficiary's share into an inherited IRA in their name; withdraw on their own schedule within the rules. Distributions are taxable (for Traditional) but never subject to the 10% penalty.
  • Lump sum: take everything at once; fully taxable in that year (for Traditional).
  • Disclaim: refuse within 9 months, so it passes to the next beneficiary.

A non-spouse beneficiary cannot roll an inherited IRA into their own IRA or make contributions to it, and cannot convert it to a Roth.

Multiple beneficiaries

Each beneficiary's share can be split into a separate inherited IRA by December 31 of the year after death, so each beneficiary manages their own share.

Eligible designated beneficiaries

Some beneficiaries qualify for life-expectancy payments instead. See Eligible designated beneficiaries.

In chat

Explain the 10-year rule and options in general terms and route to Estate Services. Planning how to spread withdrawals over 10 years is advice; refer to a tax advisor or a licensed professional.
