Options for a surviving spouse

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: surviving-spouse-options

The choices available to a spouse who inherits an IRA, and the considerations for each.

On this page

  • The options
  • Why the choice matters
  • Roth IRAs
  • Process

The options

OptionWhat it meansKey considerations
Treat as own (spousal transfer)The IRA becomes the spouse's own IRAOwn RMD rules; withdrawals before 59½ are subject to the 10% penalty
Roll over to own IRAMove the assets into the spouse's existing IRASame as treating as own
Inherited (beneficiary) IRAKeep as an inherited IRAWithdrawals at any age without the 10% penalty; RMD rules for spouse beneficiaries, which may allow delaying until the deceased would have reached RMD age
DisclaimRefuse the inheritance within 9 monthsPasses to contingent beneficiaries; must not have taken any of it
Lump-sum distributionTake everythingTaxable (except Roth); no penalty

A spouse can also start with an inherited IRA and later treat it as their own.

Why the choice matters

A younger surviving spouse who may need the money before 59½ may prefer an inherited IRA to avoid the penalty, while a spouse who doesn't need it may prefer treating it as their own to delay RMDs. These are planning considerations. Explain the options; do not recommend.

Roth IRAs

A spouse who treats an inherited Roth IRA as their own has no RMDs. As an inherited Roth, RMD rules apply.

Process

Estate Services handles the election and paperwork. In chat, explain the options and connect the spouse with Estate Services or a Retirement Specialist callback.
