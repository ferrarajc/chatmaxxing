Surviving joint owners

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: joint-survivorship

What a surviving joint owner needs to do, and how JTWROS and tenants-in-common accounts differ at death.

JTWROS

When one owner of a Joint Tenants with Rights of Survivorship account dies, the account belongs to the surviving owner(s) automatically. To re-register:

  1. The survivor provides a certified death certificate.
  2. Estate Services re-registers the account in the survivor's name (or opens a new individual account and moves the assets).
  3. The survivor should review TOD beneficiaries and automatic plans.

The surviving owner can usually continue to use the account during the process, though some transactions may be reviewed.

Tenants in common

The deceased owner's share passes to their estate (or TOD beneficiaries if registered). The surviving owner keeps their own share. The account is split once the executor provides documentation.

Surviving spouse who is also a beneficiary

Often the surviving joint owner is also the beneficiary of the deceased's IRAs. Help with both: the joint account re-registration and the IRA options. See Options for a surviving spouse.

In chat

A surviving joint owner who is a verified client can discuss the joint account. Offer condolences and route the re-registration to Estate Services.

Example

Spouses own a JTWROS account. One dies. The survivor sends a certified death certificate; the account is re-registered in the survivor's name within about a week of receiving the documents. Meanwhile, the survivor can continue to view the account and generally transact.

Automatic plans

Automatic investments from a bank account in the deceased's name may need to be changed to the survivor's bank.
