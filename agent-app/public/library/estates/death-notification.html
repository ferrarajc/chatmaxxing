When someone reports a death

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: death-notification

The first-contact procedure when a family member, executor, or beneficiary tells you an account holder has died.

On this page

  • Your role
  • Steps
  • What you can say about the accounts
  • Joint owners
  • Never

Your role

When someone reports that a client has died, your job is to respond with compassion, take the essential information, explain the next steps simply, and connect them with Estate Services. You do not settle the estate in chat.

Steps

  1. Express condolences sincerely and briefly. See Communicating with grieving clients.
  2. Identify the caller's role: spouse, child, executor, beneficiary, joint owner, attorney. Do not share any account information yet; they are not the account owner.
  3. Collect basic information: the deceased's full name, and if known, their account number or the last four digits of their SSN, and the date of death. The caller's name, relationship, and contact details.
  4. Explain what happens next:

- "We'll place a hold on the account to protect it while we review documentation." See Account holds after a death. - "Our Estate Services team will contact you within 2 business days to guide you through the process." - "You'll generally need a certified copy of the death certificate. Depending on the account, other documents may be needed; Estate Services will send a personalized checklist." See Documents needed after a death.

  1. Route the notification to Estate Services (through the death-notification referral), or offer a callback, or give the Estate Services phone route (1-800-BOB-FUND, say "inherited account").
  2. Document in ACW with the Estate Planning wrap-up code.

What you can say about the accounts

Nothing specific until documentation establishes the caller's right to know. You may describe the general process for each account type ("For retirement accounts, the named beneficiaries will have options for how to receive their share") without confirming what accounts exist or who the beneficiaries are.

Joint owners

If the person reporting the death is a joint owner of the account and is themselves a verified client, they may discuss the joint account. See Surviving joint owners.

Never

  • Never ask for documents in chat by having the person paste them.
  • Never pressure for a quick decision about inherited assets.
  • Never suggest the family move money before documentation is complete.
