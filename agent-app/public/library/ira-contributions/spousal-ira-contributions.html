Spousal IRA contributions

Section: IRA Contributions · Page ID: spousal-ira-contributions

Contribution rules for a non-working or low-earning spouse, and how to process them.

On this page

  • Rules
  • Example
  • Processing
  • Common questions

Rules

  • The couple must file a joint federal tax return.
  • Each spouse may contribute up to the annual limit ($7,500 for 2026, plus $1,100 if 50+), to IRAs in their own names.
  • Together, their contributions cannot exceed their combined taxable compensation.
  • Roth eligibility is based on the couple's joint MAGI.

Example

One spouse earns $90,000; the other has no earnings. Both are under 50. Each can contribute $7,500 for 2026, a combined $15,000, which is less than the $90,000 combined compensation.

Processing

The contribution goes into the non-working spouse's own Traditional or Roth IRA. It can be funded from a joint bank account linked to that IRA. In chat, the account owner (the non-working spouse) must be the client making the request, or the request comes from an authorized user with appropriate access.

See Spousal IRA.

Common questions

"My spouse retired last year. Can she still contribute?"

Yes, if the couple files jointly and the working spouse has enough earned income to cover both contributions.

"Does the non-working spouse need their own IRA?"

Yes. Contributions go into an IRA owned by the non-working spouse.

"Can we both contribute the catch-up?"

Each spouse aged 50 or older at year end can contribute their own catch-up.
