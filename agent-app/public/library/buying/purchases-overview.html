Purchases overview

Section: Buying · Page ID: purchases-overview

How a fund purchase works from order to settlement, the ways to place one, and the information every purchase needs.

The basics

A purchase buys shares of a BobsFunds fund in one of the client's accounts. Every purchase needs four things:

  1. Account: which of the client's accounts the shares go into.
  2. Fund: which of the 36 funds. See The BobsFunds lineup.
  3. Amount: in dollars (most common) or shares.
  4. Funding source: where the money comes from (linked bank, cash already in the account, a check, or a wire).

For IRAs, a purchase funded with new money from outside is also a contribution, and needs a tax year. See Purchases in IRAs.

Lifecycle

  1. Order received. If received in good order before 4:00 PM ET on a business day, it is priced at that day's NAV; otherwise at the next business day's NAV. See Trade cutoff and NAV pricing.
  2. Priced. After the close, the NAV is calculated and shares are credited to three decimal places.
  3. Confirmed. A trade confirmation is available the next business day. See Purchase confirmations.
  4. Settled. Mutual fund trades settle T+1. For ACH-funded purchases, the bank debit occurs within 2–3 business days.

Costs

There is no purchase fee, no sales load, and no minimum beyond $1. See Investment minimums.

What "in good order" means

An order is in good order when it includes everything needed to process it: a valid account, a valid fund, a clear amount, an available funding source, and, for IRAs, the contribution type and year. Orders missing information are not priced until the information is supplied.
