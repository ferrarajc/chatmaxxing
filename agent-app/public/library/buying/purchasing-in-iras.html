Purchases in IRAs

Section: Buying · Page ID: purchasing-in-iras

How buying in an IRA differs: contributions versus investing existing cash, tax years, limits, and rollovers.

On this page

  • Two kinds of IRA purchase
  • Checks for contributions
  • SEP-IRAs
  • Rollovers and transfers

Two kinds of IRA purchase

  1. Investing cash already in the IRA, from earlier contributions, rollovers, dividends, or sales. This is not a new contribution and does not count toward the annual limit.
  2. New money from outside (a bank, a check, a wire). This is a contribution (or a rollover or transfer, if the money comes from another retirement account). Contributions count toward the annual limit and need a tax year.

When a client says "buy $3,000 of BF500 in my Roth", ask where the money is coming from.

Checks for contributions

  • Limit: the 2026 limit is $7,500 ($8,600 at 50+), shared across all Traditional and Roth IRAs at every firm. The contributions summary in the info panel shows contributions at Bob's for the year. See The IRA contributions summary.
  • Tax year: from January 1 to April 15, a contribution can be for the current or the prior year. Ask. See Prior-year contributions (January to April).
  • Roth income limits: if the client mentions income above the phase-out, a direct Roth contribution may not be allowed. See Roth IRA income limits.
  • Age: there is no maximum age for contributions; the client needs earned income (or a working spouse).

If a proposed contribution would exceed the remaining limit, tell the client before submitting. Excess contributions incur a 6% excise tax each year until corrected. See Excess contributions and how to fix them.

SEP-IRAs

New money into a SEP-IRA is an employer contribution, which has different limits (25% of compensation up to $72,000 for 2026) and deadlines. See SEP-IRA contributions.

Rollovers and transfers

Money from a 401(k) or another IRA is not a contribution and is not limited. It must be coded correctly. See Rollovers and transfers: overview.
