Procedure: Buy / Make a Contribution (chat)

Section: Buying · Page ID: place-purchase-task

Step-by-step chat procedure for a fund purchase, every field the task collects, and the checks to make before approving.

On this page

  • What the task collects
  • Procedure
  • Common errors to avoid
  • When the purchase fails

What the task collects

FieldOptions or format
AccountOne of the client's accounts (asked only if they have more than one)
FundAny of the 36 BobsFunds tickers
Purchase amountDollar amount
Funding sourceLinked bank account, or Cash in account

Procedure

  1. Confirm what the client wants in one sentence: account, fund, amount.
  2. Let the task expert collect the details, or choose "Buy / Make a Contribution" from the task menu.
  3. Watch for these issues while details are collected:

- Unclear fund: "the S&P fund" means BF500; "the bond fund" is ambiguous (there are eight). Confirm the ticker and name. - Advice requests: if the client asks which fund to buy, the expert offers a callback. Do not suggest a fund. - Amount format: amounts such as "$4,800" or "4.8k" must be read correctly. Confirm the exact figure in the recap.

  1. When the Proposed Action appears, check:

- The account is the one the client named (use the locate button). - The fund ticker matches the fund the client described. - Cash funding: the amount is no more than the account's cash balance (not the total value). - IRA with bank funding: this is a contribution. Is it within the remaining limit? Which tax year (January–April)? If the task did not capture the tax year, ask and note it. - Time: if close to 4:00 PM ET, tell the client which day's NAV applies.

  1. Submit. The client receives a green confirmation card with a reference number.
  2. Close the loop: "Your $2,000 purchase of BF500 in your Roth IRA is placed. It will be priced at today's closing NAV, and the money will come from your bank in 2–3 business days."

Common errors to avoid

  • Funding a purchase from "cash" when the client meant to move money from another fund (that's an exchange).
  • Treating a rollover deposit as a contribution.
  • Placing the order in the wrong account when the client has two accounts of the same type.

When the purchase fails

If submission returns an error, check the account's recent transactions to see whether it was placed before retrying. See Transaction statuses.
