Returned and failed purchases

Section: Buying · Page ID: returned-purchases

Why purchases fail or are reversed, what happens to the shares, and what the client owes.

On this page

  • Reasons
  • What happens
  • Automatic investments
  • Talking to clients

Reasons

  • The bank returns the ACH debit (insufficient funds, closed account, account not authorized for debits).
  • A check is returned unpaid.
  • The order was not in good order and could not be completed (for example, an IRA contribution without a tax year).
  • The purchase would have exceeded a limit (such as the daily ACH limit).

What happens

When a payment is returned:

  1. The purchase is cancelled and the shares removed.
  2. If the NAV fell between the purchase and the cancellation, the loss is charged to the client's account. If it rose, the gain stays with the fund.
  3. A $20 returned-payment fee is charged.
  4. The client receives a notice.
  5. After two returned payments in 12 months, ACH and check purchases may be restricted for 90 days, leaving wires as the purchase method.

Automatic investments

A returned automatic investment is cancelled the same way. The plan stays active, so the next investment will be attempted. If the bank account is closed, the client should update the plan. See When an automatic investment doesn't run.

Talking to clients

Be matter-of-fact and nonjudgmental: "The bank returned the payment for your March 3 purchase, so that purchase was reversed." Offer to help them place the purchase again once funds are available.
