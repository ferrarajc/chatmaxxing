Fund distributions overview

Section: Dividends & Fund Distributions · Page ID: distributions-overview

Why funds pay distributions, the two kinds, how often, and what happens to the money.

On this page

  • Two kinds
  • What happens to the money
  • Effect on price

Why funds distribute

A mutual fund collects dividends and interest from the securities it holds and realizes gains when it sells securities at a profit. Under tax law, a fund must pass substantially all of that income and those net gains to shareholders each year. These payments are called distributions.

Two kinds

KindSourceTypical timing
Dividends (income distributions)Dividends from stocks; interest from bondsMonthly (bond funds), quarterly (most U.S. stock funds), semiannually (international funds)
Capital gain distributionsNet gains the fund realized from selling securitiesUsually once a year, in December; some years none

Index funds with low turnover usually distribute few capital gains. See Capital gain distributions.

What happens to the money

Each client chooses, per fund:

  • Reinvest (DRIP, the default): the distribution buys more shares of the same fund at that day's NAV. See Dividend reinvestment (DRIP).
  • Cash to the account: the distribution is added to the account's cash balance.
  • Cash to the bank: the distribution is sent to the linked bank by ACH.

Effect on price

On the ex-dividend date, the NAV drops by the distribution amount. The client's total value does not change because of the distribution itself. See Record, ex-dividend, reinvestment, and pay dates.

Where clients see them

Distributions appear in the account's transaction history and on statements. Annual totals are reported on Form 1099-DIV for taxable accounts. See How fund distributions are taxed.
