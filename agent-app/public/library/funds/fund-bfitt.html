What the fund does

U.S. Treasuries maturing in 3–10 years.

Tracks an index of U.S. Treasury securities with maturities between three and ten years. Backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government, the fund carries no credit risk and provides a high-quality intermediate-duration anchor for the bond sleeve of a portfolio.

In plain terms for a client: BobsFunds Intermediate Treasury (BFITT) is a intermediate treasury fund in our Fixed Income lineup. It tracks the Bloomberg US Treasury 3-10 Year Index; its turnover of 40% is typical for a bond index fund because bonds mature and are replaced, holds about 100 securities, and costs 0.04% a year, which is $4 per year on every $10,000 invested.

BobsFunds Intermediate Treasury pays distributions monthly. With dividend reinvestment (DRIP) on, which is the default, each distribution buys additional shares at that day's NAV. With DRIP off, the cash goes to the account's cash balance or the client's bank, depending on their dividend preference. See Dividend reinvestment (DRIP) and Distribution calendar.

Servicing notes

This is a bond fund. Its price moves inversely with interest rates: when rates rise, existing bonds lose value, and the longer the fund's duration the larger the move. Duration is listed in the profile table; a duration of 6 years means roughly a 6% price decline for a 1 percentage-point rise in rates, all else equal. See Bonds and interest rates and Duration.

Bond funds in the lineup pay income monthly. The fund's yield is not guaranteed and changes as the underlying bonds and market rates change. A bond fund is not a bank deposit and is not FDIC insured; it can lose value.

  • Buying, selling, and exchanging BFITT follow the standard rules: orders in good order before 4:00 PM Eastern receive that day's NAV. See Trade cutoff and NAV pricing.
  • There is no purchase fee, redemption fee, or sales load on BFITT. The only cost is the 0.04% expense ratio, which is deducted inside the fund and reflected in the NAV, never billed to the account.
  • BFITT can be held in every account type we offer: individual, joint, custodial, trust, Traditional IRA, Roth IRA, SEP-IRA, and inherited IRA.
  • Automatic investment plans into BFITT require at least $50 per investment. See Automatic investment plans.
  • The prospectus, SAI, and shareholder reports for BFITT are in the fund document library. See The prospectus.

Questions clients ask about BFITT

"Is BFITT a good investment?" / "Should I buy it?"

You cannot answer this. It is a request for a recommendation. Describe the fund factually (objective, risk level, cost, history) and offer a referral to a licensed financial advisor if the client wants guidance. Use the language in Declining advice requests.

"How much does BFITT cost?"

0.04% a year, with no transaction fees. On a $25,000 balance that is about $10.00 a year, taken gradually inside the fund rather than charged to the account.

"Why did BFITT go down?"

Explain the fund's market exposure in neutral terms (for this fund: interest-rate and credit movements). If the drop coincides with a distribution date, the NAV falls by the amount distributed; the client has not lost value if they reinvested. See "Why did my fund go down?".

"What's in it?"

Quote the allocation table above and the holdings count (100). The client portal's Research page lists top holdings.
