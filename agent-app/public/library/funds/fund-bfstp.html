What the fund does

Food, beverage, and household-goods companies.

Provides exposure to U.S. consumer staples — packaged food, beverages, household products, and retailers of everyday goods. Demand for staples is relatively inelastic, giving the sector defensive characteristics and a steady dividend, useful for dampening portfolio volatility.

In plain terms for a client: BobsFunds Consumer Staples (BFSTP) is a consumer staples fund in our Sector Equity lineup. It is managed to track its benchmark, the MSCI US IMI Consumer Staples 25/50, and trades relatively little (turnover of 5%), holds about 105 securities, and costs 0.09% a year, which is $9 per year on every $10,000 invested.

BobsFunds Consumer Staples pays distributions quarterly. With dividend reinvestment (DRIP) on, which is the default, each distribution buys additional shares at that day's NAV. Servicing notes

This is a sector fund: it concentrates on a single part of the U.S. economy. Sector funds can move very differently from the broad market, both up and down, and are typically used as a smaller tilt alongside a diversified core rather than as a complete portfolio. Say this as a description of how the product is designed, never as a recommendation.

Clients sometimes pick a sector fund after reading news about that industry. You may explain what the fund holds and point to its risk level and return history, but you may not say whether now is a good time to buy or sell. See The investment advice boundary.

  • Buying, selling, and exchanging BFSTP follow the standard rules: orders in good order before 4:00 PM Eastern receive that day's NAV. See Trade cutoff and NAV pricing.
  • There is no purchase fee, redemption fee, or sales load on BFSTP. The only cost is the 0.09% expense ratio, which is deducted inside the fund and reflected in the NAV, never billed to the account.
  • BFSTP can be held in every account type we offer: individual, joint, custodial, trust, Traditional IRA, Roth IRA, SEP-IRA, and inherited IRA.
  • Automatic investment plans into BFSTP require at least $50 per investment. See Automatic investment plans.
  • The prospectus, SAI, and shareholder reports for BFSTP are in the fund document library. See The prospectus.

Questions clients ask about BFSTP

"Is BFSTP a good investment?" / "Should I buy it?"

You cannot answer this. It is a request for a recommendation. Describe the fund factually (objective, risk level, cost, history) and offer a referral to a licensed financial advisor if the client wants guidance. Use the language in Declining advice requests.

"How much does BFSTP cost?"

0.09% a year, with no transaction fees. On a $25,000 balance that is about $22.50 a year, taken gradually inside the fund rather than charged to the account.

"Why did BFSTP go down?"

Explain the fund's market exposure in neutral terms (for this fund: the consumer staples sector). If the drop coincides with a distribution date, the NAV falls by the amount distributed; the client has not lost value if they reinvested. See "Why did my fund go down?".

"What's in it?"

Quote the allocation table above and the holdings count (105). The client portal's Research page lists top holdings.
