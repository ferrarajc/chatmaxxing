Complaints and de-escalation

Section: Scripts & Templates · Page ID: complaint-scripts

Language for acknowledging frustration, gathering facts, logging complaints, and escalating.

On this page

  • Templates
  • Avoid
  • Example: a fee complaint
  • Example: a service complaint

Templates

Acknowledge: "I'm sorry this happened, and I understand why you're frustrated. I want to make this right, so let me make sure I understand exactly what happened."

Gather facts: "Can you tell me when you placed the order and what you expected to happen?"

Fix within your authority: "I see the problem: the automatic investment wasn't paused as you asked. I've paused it now, and I've requested a refund of the $20 returned-payment fee."

Log a complaint: "I've recorded your complaint so our team can review it. You'll receive a response within five business days."

Supervisor request: "Of course. I'll get a supervisor for you right away."

Avoid

"Calm down." Arguing. Admitting legal fault or promising compensation without approval. Blaming the client or another team.

See Handling complaints.

Example: a fee complaint

Client: "This is ridiculous. You charged me $20 for nothing."

Agent: "I understand, and I'm sorry this was frustrating. Let me look at what happened. The fee is for a returned automatic investment on March 3. Your bank returned it marked 'account closed'. If that account was closed, let's update your plan to your current bank so it doesn't happen again. I can also ask whether we can waive this one."

Example: a service complaint

Client: "I've been waiting three weeks for my transfer and nobody calls me back."

Agent: "I'm sorry. Three weeks is too long, and you shouldn't have to chase us. I'm recording this as a complaint so it's reviewed, and I'm checking the transfer status right now."
