Using QCDs to satisfy RMDs

Section: Required Minimum Distributions · Page ID: rmd-and-qcd

How qualified charitable distributions count toward RMDs, timing, and coordinating with automatic RMD payments.

On this page

  • The rule
  • Timing matters
  • Coordinating with automatic RMDs
  • Reporting
  • In chat

The rule

A qualified charitable distribution made from an IRA counts toward the client's RMD for the year, up to the RMD amount, and is excluded from taxable income. Clients aged 70½ and older who give to charity often use QCDs for part or all of their RMD.

Timing matters

The first dollars distributed in a year count toward the RMD. If the client takes their full RMD in cash in January and then makes a QCD in June, the QCD does not reduce the taxable amount of the January distribution. Clients who want to use QCDs for their RMD should make the QCDs before (or instead of) other distributions.

Coordinating with automatic RMDs

If the client has automatic RMD payments and plans a QCD:

  1. They make the QCD (see Qualified charitable distributions (QCDs)).
  2. The QCD counts toward the year's RMD, so the remaining automatic payments shrink automatically.
  3. If they plan to cover the whole RMD with QCDs, they can pause automatic RMD payments for the year, then confirm the QCD total meets the RMD.

Reporting

The 1099-R reports the QCD as a normal distribution. The client reports the exclusion on their tax return and keeps the charity's acknowledgment.
