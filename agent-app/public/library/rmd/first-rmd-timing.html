Timing the first RMD

Section: Required Minimum Distributions · Page ID: first-rmd-timing

The April 1 option for the first RMD, the two-RMDs-in-one-year consequence, and how to explain the choice.

On this page

  • The rule
  • The consequence of waiting
  • Example
  • Calculating the second RMD
  • In chat

The rule

The first RMD is for the year the client reaches RMD age, but it may be delayed until April 1 of the following year (the "required beginning date"). Every later RMD is due by December 31.

The consequence of waiting

If the client delays the first RMD into the next year, they must take two RMDs in that year: the delayed first one (by April 1) and the second one (by December 31). Both are taxable in that year, which may push them into a higher bracket.

Example

A client born in 1953 turns 73 in 2026.

  • Option A: take the 2026 RMD by December 31, 2026, and the 2027 RMD by December 31, 2027.
  • Option B: take the 2026 RMD by April 1, 2027, and the 2027 RMD by December 31, 2027 (two in 2027).

Calculating the second RMD

The second-year RMD is based on the December 31 balance of the first year, which is not reduced by a first-year RMD taken in the following January–March. Retirement Specialists can assist.

In chat

Explain both options factually. Which to choose is a tax planning decision. "Some people take it in the first year to avoid doubling up; others have reasons to wait. A tax advisor can help you decide."
