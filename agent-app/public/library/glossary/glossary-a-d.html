Glossary A–D

Section: Glossary · Page ID: glossary-a-d

Definitions of terms beginning A through D.

On this page

  • A
  • B
  • C
  • D

A

ACAT

Automated Customer Account Transfer. The industry system for moving brokerage accounts between firms, started by the receiving firm. See Incoming ACAT transfers.

ACH

Automated Clearing House. The U.S. electronic bank transfer network; free, 2–3 business days. See ACH transfers.

ACW (after-chat work)

The wrap-up code, summary, and coaching generated when a chat ends, reviewed and submitted by the agent. See After-chat work (ACW).

Agent (under a POA)

The person authorized to act for another under a power of attorney. See Power of attorney.

AML

Anti-money-laundering. Laws and programs to detect and report suspicious financial activity. See Anti-money-laundering red flags.

Annualized return

The average yearly return over a multi-year period. See How performance is reported.

Asset allocation

The division of investments among stocks, bonds, and cash. See Asset allocation.

Authorized user

A person given view-only, limited, or full access to a client's account by the owner. See Authorized users and access levels.

Autopilot

The agent-workspace feature that sends AI-drafted messages on a timer, within a scope. See Autopilot: what it is and when to use it.

Average cost

The default cost basis method: the average price of all shares owned. See Cost basis methods.

B

Backdoor Roth

A nondeductible Traditional IRA contribution followed by a Roth conversion. See The "backdoor Roth" strategy.

Backup withholding

24% withholding required when a client's TIN is missing or incorrect. See Form W-9 and backup withholding.

Basis (cost basis)

What was paid for shares, used to calculate gains and losses. For IRAs, basis means nondeductible contributions.

Beneficiary

A person or entity designated to receive an account at the owner's death. See Beneficiaries: overview.

Benchmark

The index a fund tracks or is measured against. See Benchmarks and tracking.

Beta

A measure of how much a fund has moved relative to the S&P 500.

Bond

A loan to a government or company that pays interest. See Bonds and interest rates.

C

Capital gain distribution

A fund's payout of net realized gains, usually in December. See Capital gain distributions.

Catch-up contribution

The additional IRA contribution allowed at age 50+ ($1,100 for 2026). See Catch-up contributions.

CIP

Customer Identification Program; identity verification for new accounts. See Identity verification for new accounts (CIP).

Contingent beneficiary

A secondary beneficiary who inherits if all primary beneficiaries have died. See Primary and contingent beneficiaries.

Conversion (Roth)

Moving pre-tax IRA money to a Roth IRA, a taxable event. See Roth conversions: overview.

Covered shares

Shares acquired since 2012 whose basis we report to the IRS. See Covered and noncovered shares.

Custodial account

An account held for a minor under UTMA or UGMA. See Custodial accounts (UTMA/UGMA).

Cutoff

4:00 PM ET; orders received before it get that day's NAV. See Trade cutoff and NAV pricing.

D

Designated beneficiary

An individual named as an IRA beneficiary; most are subject to the 10-year rule. See Options for non-spouse beneficiaries.

Direct rollover

A rollover paid directly from a plan to an IRA. See Direct rollovers.

Distribution (fund)

A payment of dividends or capital gains by a fund. See Fund distributions overview.

Distribution (IRA)

Money taken out of an IRA. See IRA distributions overview.

Diversification

Spreading investments to reduce the impact of any single holding. See Diversification.

Dollar-cost averaging

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals. See Dollar-cost averaging.

DRIP

Dividend reinvestment; distributions buy more shares automatically. See Dividend reinvestment (DRIP).

Duration

A bond fund's sensitivity to interest-rate changes, in years. See Duration.
