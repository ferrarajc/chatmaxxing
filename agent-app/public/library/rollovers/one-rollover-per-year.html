The one-rollover-per-year rule

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: one-rollover-per-year

The limit on IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollovers, what it does not apply to, and what happens if it's broken.

On this page

  • The rule
  • What it does not apply to
  • Breaking the rule
  • In chat
  • Example
  • How to avoid it

The rule

A client may make only one IRA-to-IRA indirect (60-day) rollover in any 12-month period, counting all of their Traditional, Roth, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs together.

What it does not apply to

  • Trustee-to-trustee transfers between IRAs (unlimited).
  • Direct rollovers from employer plans to IRAs (unlimited).
  • Indirect rollovers from employer plans to IRAs.
  • Roth conversions.

Breaking the rule

A second IRA-to-IRA indirect rollover within 12 months is not a valid rollover. The distribution is taxable (and possibly penalized), and the redeposit is an excess contribution subject to 6% per year until removed. See Excess contributions and how to fix them.

In chat

If a client took an IRA distribution and wants to put it back, ask whether they have done an IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollover in the last 12 months. If yes, explain the rule and refer to Retirement Specialists before accepting the deposit as a rollover.

Example

A client takes $20,000 from their Traditional IRA in February and redeposits it in March: a valid 60-day rollover. In November they take $15,000 from a different IRA and want to redeposit it in December. That is a second IRA-to-IRA indirect rollover within 12 months and isn't allowed. The $15,000 is a taxable distribution, and redepositing it would create an excess contribution.

How to avoid it

Clients moving IRA money between institutions should use trustee-to-trustee transfers, which are unlimited. See IRA-to-IRA transfers.
