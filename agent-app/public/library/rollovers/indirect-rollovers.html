Indirect (60-day) rollovers

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: indirect-rollovers

How a 60-day rollover works, the 20% withholding trap, and how to complete one.

On this page

  • How it works
  • The withholding trap (employer plans)
  • IRA-to-IRA indirect rollovers
  • Missing the 60 days
  • How to deposit

How it works

The client receives a distribution paid to them personally and deposits it into an IRA within 60 days of receiving it. If completed in time, the distribution is not taxed.

The withholding trap (employer plans)

When an employer plan pays an eligible rollover distribution to the participant, it must withhold 20% for federal tax. To roll over the full amount, the client must deposit 100%, making up the 20% from other money. If they deposit only the 80% they received, the 20% is treated as a taxable distribution (and possibly penalized if under 59½). They get the withheld 20% back as a credit when they file their return.

Example: a $50,000 distribution; the plan withholds $10,000 and pays $40,000. To avoid tax, the client deposits $50,000 within 60 days ($40,000 plus $10,000 of their own money).

IRA-to-IRA indirect rollovers

From an IRA, withholding is 10% by default (the client may elect 0%). Only one IRA-to-IRA indirect rollover is allowed in any 12-month period. See The one-rollover-per-year rule.

Missing the 60 days

The amount becomes a taxable distribution. The IRS allows self-certification for a waiver in specific circumstances (for example, a financial institution error, serious illness, or a death in the family) using a model letter. A client who missed the deadline should consult a tax advisor; Retirement Specialists can accept a self-certification letter.

How to deposit

The client deposits the money as a rollover, not a contribution. In chat, make sure any deposit described as a rollover is coded correctly; a rollover coded as a contribution can create an excess contribution.
