Direct rollovers

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: direct-rollovers

How a direct rollover from an employer plan works, the information the plan needs, and why it's preferred.

On this page

  • How it works
  • What the plan will ask for
  • Why direct is preferred
  • Checks mailed to the client

How it works

  1. The client opens (or identifies) the receiving IRA at Bob's: a Traditional IRA for pre-tax money, a Roth IRA for Roth money.
  2. The client contacts the former employer's plan administrator (by phone or the plan's website) and requests a direct rollover to Bob's Mutual Funds.
  3. The plan sends the money by check or wire, payable to "Bob's Mutual Funds FBO [client name]", referencing the client's IRA account number.
  4. We deposit it as a rollover contribution and invest it as the client directs (or hold it as cash until they choose).

What the plan will ask for

ItemWhat to provide
Receiving institutionBob's Mutual Funds
PayeeBob's Mutual Funds FBO [client's full name]
Account numberThe client's Bob's IRA account number
Account typeTraditional IRA or Roth IRA
Mailing address for checksBob's Mutual Funds, PO Box 2025, Malvern, PA 19355 (or the overnight address)
Wire instructionsFirst National Bank of Funds, ABA 031000503, for credit to the client's account
Letter of acceptanceSome plans require one; request it from Retirement Specialists

Why direct is preferred

  • No 20% mandatory withholding.
  • No 60-day deadline risk.
  • No risk of losing the one-rollover-per-year allowance (which applies only to IRA-to-IRA indirect rollovers anyway).

Checks mailed to the client

Some plans send the "direct rollover" check to the participant, still payable to Bob's FBO the client. That is still a direct rollover: the client simply forwards the check to us, with their account number. See Handling rollover checks.
