@page prospectus | The prospectus
@summary What a fund prospectus contains, when clients must receive it, and how to send or explain one.

## What it is

The prospectus is the fund's primary legal disclosure document, filed with the SEC. Every fund has one, updated at least annually. Clients should read it before investing; we deliver it (or a summary prospectus) at the time of a client's first purchase of a fund.

## What's in it

- **Investment objective** and **principal strategies**: what the fund is trying to do and how.
- **Principal risks**: market, interest-rate, credit, foreign, sector concentration, and others.
- **Fees and expenses**: the expense ratio and any other costs (our funds have no loads or redemption fees).
- **Performance**: a bar chart of calendar-year returns and a table comparing returns to the benchmark.
- **Management**: the adviser and portfolio managers.
- **Purchase and sale of shares**, including the frequent-trading policy.
- **Distributions and taxes**.

## Summary prospectus

A short version (a few pages) covering the key facts, with the full prospectus available online. Clients usually receive the summary.

## Where clients find it

Each fund's Research page has links to the prospectus, SAI, and shareholder reports. Printed copies can be mailed free on request. See [[document-requests]].

## How to use it in chat

When a client asks about a fund's risks or strategy in detail, quote the fund profile in this library and send the prospectus link. Never paraphrase risk in a way that minimizes it.

@page sai | Statement of Additional Information (SAI)
@summary What the SAI contains and when clients need it.

## What it is

The Statement of Additional Information is a supplement to the prospectus with more detailed information: the fund's investment policies and restrictions, trustees and officers, portfolio manager compensation and fund ownership, the custodian and auditor, tax matters, and how the NAV is calculated.

## Who asks for it

Usually professionals: financial advisors, attorneys, or academically minded clients. It is available on each fund's Research page and by mail on request.

## In chat

Send the link. You don't need to explain its contents in detail; refer specific questions about governance or policies to the document.

## Typical contents

- Fund history and organization.
- Investment policies, permitted investments, and fundamental restrictions (for example, limits on borrowing and concentration).
- Board of trustees and officers, with their backgrounds and compensation.
- Portfolio managers' other accounts, compensation structure, and fund ownership ranges.
- Control persons and principal holders.
- The investment adviser, distributor, custodian, transfer agent, and independent auditor.
- Brokerage allocation and soft dollars.
- Net asset value calculation methodology.
- Tax status of the fund.
- Proxy voting policies.

## When it's useful

Advisors doing due diligence, clients interested in governance, and questions about specific restrictions ("Can the fund use derivatives?") are answered in the SAI.

@page shareholder-reports | Annual and semiannual reports
@summary What shareholder reports show, when they're published, and how clients receive them.

## What they are

Each fund publishes:

- An **annual report** for its fiscal year, with audited financial statements, a complete list of holdings, a management discussion of performance, and expense information.
- A **semiannual report** for the first six months of the fiscal year, with unaudited financial statements and holdings.

Shorter "tailored shareholder reports" summarize performance, expenses, and holdings in a few pages; the full financial statements are available online.

## Delivery

Clients who own a fund receive the tailored shareholder report by email (paperless) or mail. Households with several members owning the same fund may receive one copy (householding) unless they ask for separate copies.

## In chat

Send the link from the fund's Research page. Questions about holdings: the fund profile and Research page list the top holdings; the reports list everything.

## Fiscal years

Our funds' fiscal years end on various dates (for example, December 31 for the U.S. equity funds). The annual report covers the fiscal year; the semiannual report covers its first half.

## What clients look for

- **Holdings**: the full list as of the report date.
- **Expense example**: what a $1,000 investment would have cost during the period.
- **Management discussion**: why the fund performed as it did.
- **Financial highlights**: per-share data over five years, including total return and expense ratio.

## Householding

To reduce mail, households receive one copy of a report per address. A client who wants their own copy can ask; we resume individual delivery within 30 days.

@page performance-reporting | How performance is reported
@summary Total return, standard periods, annualized versus cumulative returns, and the rules for quoting performance.

## Total return

Fund performance is reported as **total return**: the change in NAV plus distributions, assuming they were reinvested. It is net of the expense ratio.

## Standard periods

| Period | Notes |
|---|---|
| Year to date (YTD) | From January 1 |
| 1 year | Trailing 12 months |
| 3, 5, 10 years | **Annualized** (average annual return) |
| Since inception | Annualized |
| Calendar years | Each full year |

## Annualized versus cumulative

A 3-year annualized return of 8% means the fund grew at an average of 8% per year, about 26% cumulatively. Clients sometimes confuse the two; clarify which you are quoting.

## Where to find current figures

The fund's Research page shows YTD and trailing returns, refreshed nightly. Calendar-year returns are on each fund profile page in this library.

## Rules for quoting performance

- Always include the time period and the date: "As of September 30, 2026, BF500's one-year return was..."
- Always add: "Past performance doesn't guarantee future results."
- Quote standard periods, not cherry-picked ones.
- Never project future returns.
- Never compare our fund's performance to a competitor's fund in a promotional way.

See [[communications-standards]].

## Personal performance

A client's own account performance (their personal rate of return) depends on when they invested. The portal's Performance tab shows the client's personal return. It can differ from the fund's return; that is normal.

@page total-return-vs-price | Total return versus price change
@summary Why a fund's price change understates its return, especially for funds that distribute a lot.

## The difference

A fund's NAV change ignores distributions. When a fund pays a distribution, its NAV drops by that amount. So:

- **Price return** = change in NAV only.
- **Total return** = change in NAV plus distributions reinvested.

For bond funds, which pay out most of their return as income, price return can be near zero or negative while total return is positive.

## Example

BFBI's NAV starts the year at $98.30 and ends at $98.10 (price return about −0.2%). It paid about $2.10 per share in monthly dividends during the year. Its total return is about +1.9%.

## Client questions

### "My bond fund price hasn't moved in years."
Explain that bond funds pay their return mostly as monthly dividends; with reinvestment on, the client owns more shares over time.

### "The chart shows a drop but the return is positive."
The chart may show NAV only. Check the total-return figures.

## Stock funds too

Stock funds that pay larger dividends (BFHDY, BFUTL) also show a noticeable gap between price return and total return. Growth funds (BFGR) pay small dividends, so the gap is small.

## Where to see total return

Fund profile return tables and the Research page's performance tab show total returns. Price charts show NAV only unless the "include distributions" option is selected.

@page yields-explained | Yields
@summary The kinds of yield quoted for funds, what each means, and how to explain that yields are not guaranteed.

## Types of yield

| Yield | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Distribution yield (trailing 12-month) | The fund's distributions over the past 12 months divided by the current NAV. Shown on fund profiles in this library |
| SEC 30-day yield | A standardized calculation of the fund's net investment income over the last 30 days, annualized. Useful for comparing bond funds |
| Dividend yield (for stock funds) | The income from the stocks the fund holds |

## Yields vary

Yields are not guaranteed and change with market rates and the fund's holdings. A bond fund's yield is not like a CD's fixed rate: the fund has no maturity, and its price changes.

## Yield versus return

Yield is income only. Total return includes price changes. A fund can have a 4% yield and a negative total return in a year when rates rise.

## In chat

Quote the yield shown with its type and date. "BFST's trailing 12-month distribution yield is 4.58%; that's what it paid over the past year relative to today's price. It isn't a guaranteed rate."

## Example

BFBI shows a trailing 12-month distribution yield of 3.82%. A client with $10,000 in BFBI received about $382 in dividends over the past year. This year's dividends could be higher or lower.

## Why yields change

As bonds in the fund mature and are replaced at current market rates, the fund's yield moves toward those rates. When rates fall, yields drift lower; when rates rise, they drift higher.

@page fund-fact-data-sources | Where fund data comes from
@summary Which fund figures are static catalog data and which are refreshed nightly, so you know what's current.

## Two kinds of data

| Data | Source | Updated |
|---|---|---|
| Name, ticker, category, expense ratio, benchmark, minimum, risk level, description | Fund catalog | When the prospectus changes |
| Holdings count, allocations, calendar-year returns, recent distributions, yield, beta, standard deviation | Fund catalog | Periodically |
| Current NAV, daily change, YTD and trailing returns, price charts | Nightly market data refresh | Every business night |

The fund profile pages in this library are generated from the same catalog the client portal uses. For anything time-sensitive (today's price, YTD return), use the fund's Research page in the portal.

## If numbers disagree

If the Research page and a library page disagree on a time-sensitive figure, the Research page is more current. If they disagree on a catalog figure (expense ratio, benchmark), report it as a content issue. Older client-facing help pages may show outdated figures for the original six funds.

## Nightly refresh

Market data (NAV, returns, price history) refreshes every night after the markets close. If the nightly refresh is delayed, the Research page continues to show the last good data; it never shows partial or broken data.

## Questions

### "Why does the Research page show yesterday's price?"
Before about 6:00 PM ET, the latest NAV is the previous business day's. Today's NAV appears after the close.

### "The return on the website doesn't match my statement."
Fund returns and the client's personal return differ. See [[performance-reporting]].

@page document-requests | Requests for fund documents
@summary How to send prospectuses, reports, and other fund documents by link or mail.

## By link

Every fund's Research page has links to the summary prospectus, full prospectus, SAI, and shareholder reports. Send the link in chat.

## By mail

Clients can request printed copies free of charge. Note the request (fund, document, mailing address on file) and route it to Client Services fulfillment; documents are mailed within 3 business days.

## Other documents

| Request | Where |
|---|---|
| Account forms (beneficiary, transfer, name change, QCD) | Forms page in the portal |
| Privacy notice | Portal footer |
| Fee schedule | Help → Fees |
| Proxy materials | Mailed or emailed to shareholders when there is a vote |
| Statements, confirmations, tax forms | My Account |

## Prospectus delivery rules

Clients receive a summary prospectus when they first buy a fund, and updated prospectuses annually. Electronic delivery is used for paperless clients. A client can always request a free printed copy.

## Proxy materials

When a fund holds a shareholder vote (for example, to elect trustees or change a fundamental policy), shareholders of record receive proxy materials and can vote online, by phone, or by mail. Questions about a specific proxy can be answered from the proxy statement.

## Historical documents

Prospectuses and reports for prior years are available from Client Services fulfillment on request.
