@page exchanges-overview | Exchanges overview
@summary What an exchange is, how it is priced, what it can and cannot do, and how it differs from a sale and a purchase.

## Definition

An exchange moves money from one BobsFunds fund to another **within the same account**. It is a sale of the first fund and a purchase of the second, processed together at the same day's NAVs, so the money is never out of the market overnight.

## Rules

- Both funds must be in the same account. Moving money between two accounts (for example, from a taxable account to a Roth IRA) is not an exchange. See [[exchange-rules-limits]].
- The exchange is priced at both funds' NAVs for the trade date (that day if before 4:00 PM ET).
- There is no fee and no minimum beyond $1.
- An exchange can be for a dollar amount, a number of shares, or all shares of the "from" fund.
- An exchange into a new fund opens a new position; an exchange into a fund already held adds to it.

## Taxes

- **IRAs**: an exchange has no tax effect.
- **Taxable accounts**: an exchange is a sale and can create a capital gain or loss. See [[exchanges-tax]].

## Channels

Online (Transactions → Exchange), mobile app, chat (see [[exchange-funds-task]]), or phone.

## Exchanges and the frequent-trading policy

Exchanges count toward round trips. See [[frequent-trading-policy]].

@page exchange-rules-limits | Exchange rules and limits
@summary What can be exchanged, between which accounts, and the situations where an exchange isn't possible.

## Allowed

- Any BobsFunds fund to any other BobsFunds fund in the same account.
- Partial or full exchanges.
- Several exchanges on the same day (each is a separate transaction).

## Not allowed or not an exchange

| Request | Why not | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Move money from a taxable account to an IRA | Different accounts and tax types | Sell in the taxable account and make an IRA contribution (subject to limits) |
| Move money from an IRA to a taxable account | That is a distribution | Request a distribution |
| Move from a Traditional IRA to a Roth IRA | That is a conversion | See [[roth-conversion-overview]] |
| Move between two of the client's taxable accounts | Different accounts | A journal transfer between accounts (Account Services), or sell and buy |
| Exchange into another company's fund | We hold only BobsFunds | Not available |
| Exchange while purchase payment is pending | Allowed within the account | The redemption hold still applies to withdrawals |

## Restrictions

A client restricted under the frequent-trading policy cannot exchange into the restricted fund for 30 days. A foreign address can block exchanges into funds as new purchases. See [[non-us-persons]].

@page frequent-trading-policy | Frequent trading (market timing) policy
@summary How the funds limit short-term round trips, what counts, the 30-day block, and what's exempt.

## Why the policy exists

Frequent short-term trading in and out of a fund raises costs for long-term shareholders and can dilute their returns. The funds' prospectuses describe a policy to discourage it.

## The rule

A **round trip** is a purchase or exchange into a fund followed by a sale or exchange out of the same fund within **30 calendar days**. After a round trip, the client is **blocked from buying or exchanging into that fund for 30 calendar days** (in that account and any account with the same owner).

## What is exempt

- Automatic investment plans and systematic withdrawal plans.
- Dividend and capital gain reinvestments.
- Required minimum distributions.
- Redemptions to pay account fees.
- Transactions in BFST (Short-Term Treasury), which is designed for short-term use.
- Corrections of errors made by Bob's Mutual Funds.

## In chat

If a client asks to buy back into a fund they sold within 30 days, check whether the earlier trade created a round trip. If the system blocks the purchase, explain:

"Our funds limit short-term trading to protect long-term shareholders. Because you bought and then sold this fund within 30 days, new purchases into it are paused until [date]. You can still buy any other fund."

Do not help clients work around the policy (for example, buying in a different account). Repeated attempts to time the market may lead to wider trading restrictions.

## Talking about market timing

Clients who trade frequently in response to news may be anxious. Avoid commentary on their strategy; if they want guidance, offer a callback. See [[market-downturn-conversations]].

@page exchanges-tax | Tax effects of exchanges
@summary Why exchanges in taxable accounts are taxable sales, and how to explain this without giving tax advice.

## Taxable accounts

An exchange is treated as a sale of the "from" fund. If the shares are worth more than their cost basis, the client realizes a capital gain; if less, a loss. The exchange appears on Form 1099-B for the year. See [[redemption-tax-basics]].

Clients are often surprised by this, because the money stayed invested. Explain it before submitting an exchange in a taxable account if the client seems unaware: "Just so you know, in a taxable account an exchange counts as a sale for tax purposes, so it may result in a capital gain or loss."

## IRAs

Exchanges inside a Traditional, Roth, SEP, or inherited IRA have no tax effect.

## Wash sales

If an exchange sells shares at a loss and the client buys the same fund (or a "substantially identical" one) within 30 days before or after, part or all of the loss may be disallowed under the wash-sale rule. Whether two different index funds are substantially identical is a tax question for the client's advisor. See [[wash-sales]].

## Cost basis method

The cost basis method chosen (average cost by default) determines which shares' basis is used. Clients who want to choose specific lots must set specific identification before the exchange. See [[cost-basis-methods]].

@page exchange-funds-task | Procedure: Exchange Between Funds (chat)
@summary Step-by-step chat procedure for an exchange, the fields collected, and the checks before approving.

## What the task collects

| Field | Options or format |
|---|---|
| Account | One of the client's accounts (asked if more than one) |
| Fund to exchange out of | A fund held in that account |
| Fund to exchange into | Any BobsFunds fund |
| Amount to exchange | A dollar amount, or the full balance of the "from" fund |

## Procedure

1. Confirm the request in one sentence: "You'd like to move $3,000 from BFGR into BFBI in your Traditional IRA. Is that right?"
2. Let the task expert collect the details, or choose Exchange Between Funds from the task menu.
3. Check the Proposed Action:
   - "From" fund is held in the account and the amount is no more than its value.
   - "To" fund is the one the client described. Watch out for similar names (BFST Short-Term Treasury versus BFSTC Short-Term Corporate versus BFSTP Consumer Staples).
   - "From" and "to" are not the same fund.
   - In a taxable account, the client knows an exchange is a taxable sale. See [[exchanges-tax]].
   - The exchange does not violate the frequent-trading policy. See [[frequent-trading-policy]].
4. Submit. The client receives a green confirmation card.
5. Tell the client the exchange will be priced at today's NAVs (if before 4:00 PM ET).

## Requests that aren't exchanges

- Moving between accounts: see [[exchange-rules-limits]].
- "Rebalance my account": see [[rebalancing-requests]].
- "Move everything to safety": an emotional request in a falling market. Process it if it is clearly what the client wants, without commentary; if they ask your opinion, see [[market-downturn-conversations]].

@summary How to help a client who wants to rebalance: what you can calculate, what you can't recommend, and how to process the exchanges.

## What rebalancing means

Rebalancing is selling some of what has grown and buying some of what has lagged, to return a portfolio to a chosen mix (for example, 60% stock funds and 40% bond funds). See [[rebalancing]] for the educational explanation.

## What you can do

- If the client states their **own** target mix, you can show them their current mix from the holdings and calculate the exchanges needed to reach their target. That is arithmetic, not advice.
- Process the exchanges they choose, one exchange per Proposed Action.
- Explain that exchanges in a taxable account can create capital gains.

## What you cannot do

- Choose the target mix for them, or say whether their mix is appropriate.
- Recommend which funds to rebalance into.
- Recommend how often to rebalance.

## Example

The client says: "I want to be 70% BF500 and 30% BFBI. I have $85,000 in BF500 and $15,000 in BFBI." Total $100,000; target $70,000 and $30,000. You can say: "To reach 70/30, you'd exchange $15,000 from BF500 into BFBI. Would you like me to set that up?"

## Wrap-up

Use the Rebalancing Discussion wrap-up code. If the client wanted advice on the mix, offer a callback and use Investment Advice.
