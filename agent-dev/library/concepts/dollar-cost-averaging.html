Dollar-cost averaging

Section: Investing Concepts (Educational) · Page ID: dollar-cost-averaging

How investing a fixed amount regularly works, its effect on average cost, and how to describe it accurately.

On this page

  • What it is
  • The effect
  • What it doesn't do
  • How to discuss

What it is

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals regardless of the price. Automatic investment plans do this automatically.

The effect

A fixed amount buys more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, so the average cost per share is lower than the average price over the period.

What it doesn't do

DCA does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss in a declining market. Studies show that investing a lump sum at once has historically produced higher returns more often than not, because markets have risen more often than fallen; DCA reduces the regret of investing just before a decline. That is a general observation, not advice.

How to discuss

Explain the mechanics. Whether to invest a lump sum or spread it out is the client's decision.
