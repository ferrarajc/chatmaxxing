Aggregating RMDs across accounts

Section: Required Minimum Distributions · Page ID: rmd-aggregation

How clients with several IRAs can satisfy their RMDs, and what cannot be combined.

On this page

  • IRAs
  • What cannot be combined
  • Our automatic RMD service
  • Explaining it

IRAs

The RMD is calculated separately for each Traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRA, but the client may take the total from any one or more of their IRAs. Example: a client has two IRAs with RMDs of $8,000 and $4,000; they can take $12,000 from either one.

This applies across firms: a client with an IRA at another firm can take that IRA's RMD from their Bob's IRA, or vice versa. We can only track distributions from accounts at Bob's.

What cannot be combined

  • Employer plans (401(k), 403(b)): each plan's RMD must come from that plan. 403(b)s can be aggregated with each other, but not with IRAs.
  • Inherited IRAs cannot be combined with the client's own IRAs. Inherited IRAs from the same original owner can be combined with each other.
  • Spouses cannot combine: each spouse satisfies their own RMDs.
  • Roth IRAs owned by the original owner have no RMD.

Our automatic RMD service

Our RMD service distributes each IRA's RMD from that IRA. If a client wants to take one IRA's RMD from another, they can turn off the automatic RMD for one account and take a larger distribution from the other. Our year-end notices may still show an RMD outstanding on the first account; the client is responsible for the total.

Explaining it

"Each IRA has its own RMD, but the IRS lets you take the total from whichever IRAs you choose. Our system tracks each account separately, so if you take it all from one, the other account will still show an RMD remaining; that's expected."
