First-contact checklist

Section: Start Here · Page ID: first-contact-checklist

The steps to take in the first two minutes of every chat so nothing is missed later.

The checklist

  1. Read the intent summary. When a chat is escalated, the workspace shows an AI-generated intent label and summary of what the client asked the virtual assistant. Read it before you type, so you do not ask the client to repeat themselves.
  2. Check for a continued chat. If the column shows a prior transcript marked as a continued conversation, skim the previous recap. The client expects you to remember. See Continued chats.
  3. Greet the client. The suggested greeting is drafted from the intent. Edit it if it does not fit, then send. See Greetings.
  4. Confirm identity. Clients who reach chat from inside the logged-in portal have already authenticated. If anything suggests you are not talking to the account owner (a different name, a third party, a request that does not fit the profile), follow Verifying client identity.
  5. Scan the client info panel. Note the accounts (types, IDs, balances), any IRA contribution summary, and anything unusual such as a pending transaction or an account on hold.
  6. Confirm the request in one sentence. "It sounds like you'd like to move $2,000 from your BF500 holding into the bond fund in your Roth IRA. Is that right?" This catches misunderstandings before they become errors.
  7. Choose the path. Is this a question (answer it), a task (start the matching task expert or let autopilot collect details), or a specialist request (route it)?
  8. Watch the clock. Keep the response timer out of amber while you work.

Signals that require extra care

  • The client mentions a death, divorce, diagnosis, or financial hardship. Slow down; see Communicating with grieving clients.
  • The client reports a transaction they did not make, or an email or call asking for their password. Treat as possible fraud; see Suspected account takeover or unauthorized activity.
  • The client sounds confused about recent instructions, or a third party is directing them. See Elder financial exploitation and vulnerable clients.
  • The client asks what to buy or sell. That is advice; see The investment advice boundary.
  • The client is angry or uses the word "complaint". See Handling complaints.
