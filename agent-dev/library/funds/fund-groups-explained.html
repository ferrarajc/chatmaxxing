How the lineup is organized

Section: Fund Lineup · Page ID: fund-groups-explained

What distinguishes the four fund groups and the style categories within U.S. equity.

On this page

  • The four groups
  • Size and style
  • Total market versus 500
  • Bond funds by maturity

The four groups

  • U.S. Equity: funds holding stocks of U.S. companies. They differ by company size (large, mid, small), by style (growth, value, blend), and by special focus (dividends, ESG screening).
  • Sector Equity: funds concentrated in one of the eleven sectors of the U.S. economy, such as technology or utilities. Narrower and usually more volatile than broad funds.
  • International: funds holding stocks of companies outside the United States, broadly (BFIN, BFDEV) or by region (BFEUR, BFPAC) or in emerging markets (BFEMG).
  • Fixed Income: bond funds, differing by issuer (U.S. Treasury, corporate, or a broad mix) and by maturity (short, intermediate, long).

Size and style

U.S. stock funds are described with a "style box": size (Large, Mid, Small) and style (Value, Blend, Growth).

  • Growth funds hold companies whose earnings and sales are expected to grow faster than average; they tend to have higher prices relative to earnings and lower dividends.
  • Value funds hold companies that look inexpensive relative to earnings, book value, or dividends; they tend to pay higher dividends.
  • Blend funds hold both.

Large companies are generally more established and less volatile; small companies offer more growth potential with more volatility. These are general characteristics, not predictions.

Total market versus 500

BF500 holds the 500 largest U.S. companies. BFTM holds the whole U.S. market, about 3,650 companies, including mid and small caps. Because the largest companies dominate both by weight, their returns are usually similar. See BobsFunds 500 Index (BF500) and BobsFunds Total Market Index (BFTM).

Bond funds by maturity

Shorter-maturity bond funds (BFST, BFSTC) are less sensitive to interest rates and usually yield less; longer funds (BFLTT, BFLTC) are more sensitive and usually yield more. Treasury funds carry no credit risk; corporate funds carry credit risk and usually yield more. See Bonds and interest rates.
