Systematic withdrawal plans

Section: Automatic Investing & Withdrawals · Page ID: systematic-withdrawals-overview

How recurring withdrawals work, frequencies, delivery, which shares are sold, and taxes.

On this page

  • What it is
  • Plan details
  • Taxes
  • A note on sustainability

What it is

A systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) sells a fixed dollar amount from the client's account on a schedule and sends the money to their bank or by check. Retirees use it to create a regular "paycheck" from their savings.

Plan details

SettingOptions
AmountMinimum $50 per withdrawal
FrequencyMonthly, quarterly, or annually
Start dateThe first withdrawal date
DeliveryDirect deposit (ACH) to a linked bank, or check by mail
Funds to sellProportionally across holdings, or from specific funds the client chooses
Tax withholding (IRAs)Federal (default 10% for Traditional and SEP IRAs) and state

Taxes

  • Taxable accounts: each withdrawal sells shares and can produce capital gains or losses, reported on Form 1099-B.
  • Traditional and SEP IRAs: each withdrawal is a taxable distribution, reported on Form 1099-R, with federal withholding unless the client elects 0%.
  • Roth IRAs: withdrawals of contributions are tax-free; earnings depend on whether the distribution is qualified. See Roth IRA distribution rules.

A note on sustainability

Withdrawals that exceed the account's returns will gradually use up the principal. You may describe this arithmetic neutrally if the client asks how long the money will last, but planning a sustainable withdrawal rate is advice; offer a callback.

RMDs

A client who must take RMDs can use a SWP sized to meet their RMD, or the dedicated RMD settings, which calculate the amount automatically. See Procedure: Update RMD Settings (chat).
