Rolling over a 401(k)

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: rollover-401k

The step-by-step process for moving a former employer's 401(k) into a Bob's IRA, and the questions clients ask.

Steps

  1. Check the account type: pre-tax money → Traditional IRA; Roth 401(k) money → Roth IRA; after-tax contributions can go to a Roth IRA (see After-tax 401(k) money). Many 401(k)s have more than one type.
  2. Open the receiving IRA(s) at Bob's if the client doesn't have them. See Procedure: Open a New Account (chat).
  3. Start the rollover in chat with Procedure: Roll Over From Another Institution (chat), which records the expected rollover and gives the client our receiving details.
  4. Contact the plan: the client calls the plan administrator or uses its website to request a direct rollover, giving our details. See Direct rollovers.
  5. The plan sends the money: typically within 5–15 business days.
  6. Invest: when the money arrives, it is invested as the client directed, or held as cash for the client to invest.

Common questions

"Do I have to roll it over?"

No. Leaving it in the plan, rolling to an IRA, moving it to a new employer's plan, or cashing out are all options with different consequences. Explaining the options is fine; recommending one is advice. Offer a callback for guidance.

"Will I be taxed?"

Not on a direct rollover of pre-tax money to a Traditional IRA or Roth money to a Roth IRA.

"Can I roll over while I still work there?"

Usually not, though some plans allow "in-service" rollovers at 59½. The plan decides.

"My 401(k) has company stock."

Employer stock can have special tax treatment (net unrealized appreciation) that is lost if rolled into an IRA. Refer the client to a tax advisor before they roll over company stock.

"I'm 55 and might need the money."

The age-55 separation-from-service penalty exception applies to the 401(k), not to an IRA. See Exceptions to the early-withdrawal penalty.
