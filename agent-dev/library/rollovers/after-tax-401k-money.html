After-tax 401(k) money

Section: Rollovers & Transfers · Page ID: after-tax-401k-money

How after-tax (non-Roth) contributions in a 401(k) can be split between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA.

On this page

  • What it is
  • Splitting the rollover
  • Why it matters
  • In chat
  • Example
  • Questions to send to the plan

What it is

Some 401(k) plans allow after-tax contributions, separate from pre-tax and Roth contributions. The contributions have already been taxed; the earnings on them have not.

Splitting the rollover

Under IRS rules, a client rolling over a distribution that includes after-tax contributions can direct:

  • The after-tax contributions (basis) to a Roth IRA, tax-free, and
  • The pre-tax amounts (including earnings on the after-tax money) to a Traditional IRA, tax-free.

This must be arranged with the plan, which issues separate checks, and the client must provide both account numbers.

Why it matters

Getting after-tax money into a Roth IRA lets future earnings grow tax-free. Done incorrectly (for example, the whole amount to a Traditional IRA), the after-tax basis must be tracked on Form 8606 forever.

In chat

Explain that the split is possible and that the plan administrator handles the allocation. Refer complex cases to Retirement Specialists. Do not advise whether to do it.

Example

A client has a 401(k) with $300,000: $250,000 pre-tax, $40,000 of after-tax contributions, and $10,000 of earnings on the after-tax contributions. They can direct $40,000 to a Roth IRA and $260,000 (pre-tax plus the earnings) to a Traditional IRA, tax-free.

Questions to send to the plan

Clients should ask their plan for a breakdown of pre-tax, Roth, and after-tax balances, and whether it will issue separate checks for each destination.
