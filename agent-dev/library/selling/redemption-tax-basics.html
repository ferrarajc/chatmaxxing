Taxes on sales in taxable accounts

Section: Selling · Page ID: redemption-tax-basics

How capital gains and losses arise when a client sells in a taxable account, and how much you may explain.

On this page

  • The basics
  • Cost basis
  • Exchanges are sales
  • Losses
  • What you can and cannot say

The basics

In a taxable account, selling or exchanging shares realizes a capital gain or loss: the sale proceeds minus the cost basis of the shares sold.

  • Shares held more than one year produce long-term gains or losses, taxed at lower rates (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income, plus 3.8% net investment income tax for some high earners).
  • Shares held one year or less produce short-term gains, taxed as ordinary income.

Cost basis

Basis is what the client paid for the shares, including reinvested dividends. We track basis for shares bought since 2012 (covered shares) and report it on Form 1099-B. Our default method is average cost; clients can choose FIFO or specific identification before a sale. See Cost basis methods.

Exchanges are sales

An exchange in a taxable account is a sale of one fund and a purchase of another, so it can produce a gain or loss. See Tax effects of exchanges.

Losses

Losses offset gains, and up to $3,000 of net loss a year can offset ordinary income, with the rest carried forward. Buying the same fund within 30 days before or after a loss sale can trigger the wash-sale rule. See The wash-sale rule.

What you can and cannot say

You may explain these rules and show the client their unrealized gain or loss and cost basis in the portal (Account → Holdings → Cost Basis). You may not tell the client how a sale will affect their taxes or whether they should sell for tax reasons. See Tax information versus tax advice.
