International money movement

Section: Money Movement · Page ID: international-money-movement

What we can and cannot do with foreign banks, currencies, and clients abroad.

On this page

  • What we don't do
  • Incoming international wires
  • Clients moving abroad
  • U.S. citizens abroad
  • Incoming foreign wires
  • Currency

What we don't do

  • We do not send wires to foreign banks.
  • We do not accept checks drawn on foreign banks.
  • We do not convert currencies. All accounts are in U.S. dollars.

Incoming international wires

A client abroad can send a wire from a foreign bank through the bank's U.S. correspondent to our receiving bank in U.S. dollars, using our standard instructions. Intermediary banks may deduct fees, so the amount received can be less than sent. Incoming foreign wires get additional anti-money-laundering review and may take an extra business day.

Clients moving abroad

Clients need a U.S. bank account to receive electronic payments. See Non-U.S. persons and clients living abroad.

U.S. citizens abroad

Citizens living abroad often keep a U.S. bank account for transfers. Withdrawals go only to U.S. banks in the owner's name.

Incoming foreign wires

The sending bank should include our receiving bank's details and the client's account number and name. Foreign wires can take an extra business day for compliance review. Intermediary fees may reduce the amount received.

Currency

All accounts are in U.S. dollars. If the client sends a foreign-currency wire, the conversion happens at the sending or intermediary bank, at their rate.
