Eligible designated beneficiaries

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: eligible-designated-beneficiaries

Who qualifies to stretch inherited IRA distributions over their life expectancy, and the special rule for minor children.

On this page

  • Categories
  • What EDBs can do
  • Documentation
  • Grandchildren
  • Examples
  • Choosing life expectancy or 10 years

Categories

An eligible designated beneficiary (EDB) is:

  1. The owner's surviving spouse.
  2. The owner's minor child (under 21). When the child turns 21, the 10-year rule begins.
  3. A disabled individual (as defined by the IRS).
  4. A chronically ill individual.
  5. An individual not more than 10 years younger than the owner (for example, a sibling close in age).

Certain trusts for disabled or chronically ill beneficiaries can also qualify.

What EDBs can do

Take annual RMDs over their own single life expectancy, instead of emptying the account within 10 years. They can always take more than the minimum.

Documentation

Disability or chronic illness status requires documentation. Estate Services collects it.

Grandchildren

A grandchild is not an EDB on the basis of being a minor (only the owner's own minor children qualify). Grandchildren are subject to the 10-year rule.

Examples

  • A 68-year-old sister inheriting from her 72-year-old brother: not more than 10 years younger, so an EDB.
  • A 45-year-old son inheriting from his 75-year-old mother: not an EDB unless disabled or chronically ill; 10-year rule.
  • A 12-year-old daughter of the owner: an EDB until 21, then the 10-year rule.

Choosing life expectancy or 10 years

Some EDBs may elect the 10-year rule instead, which can be simpler. That choice is planning; refer to Estate Services and a tax advisor.
