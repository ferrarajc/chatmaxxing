Estate settlement timelines

Section: Death, Estates & Inheritance · Page ID: estate-timelines

How long each part of an estate settlement typically takes and how to set expectations.

On this page

  • Typical timeline
  • Factors that extend it
  • Setting expectations
  • Example
  • Keeping families informed

Typical timeline

StepTypical time
Death reported, hold placedSame day
Estate Services first contactWithin 2 business days
Personalized document checklist sentWithin 1–2 business days of first contact
Gathering documents (family side)Days to weeks; death certificates can take 1–3 weeks
Review of complete documents3–5 business days
Transfer of assets once approved5–10 business days
Probate (if needed)Months, set by the court

Factors that extend it

  • Missing or uncertified documents.
  • Multiple beneficiaries who must each respond.
  • Beneficiaries who are minors, incapacitated, or cannot be located.
  • Disputes among beneficiaries or legal challenges.
  • Trusts with complex terms.

Setting expectations

Give the family the next step and a realistic range, and let them know Estate Services will keep them informed. Do not promise a completion date.

Example

DayEvent
Day 1Daughter reports her mother's death in chat
Day 2Estate Services calls and emails a checklist
Day 12Daughter uploads certified death certificate and claim forms
Day 16Review complete
Day 22Inherited IRA established and funded

Keeping families informed

Estate Services provides updates, but families sometimes contact chat for status. You can check whether documents have been received and the case status, and give the expected next step.
