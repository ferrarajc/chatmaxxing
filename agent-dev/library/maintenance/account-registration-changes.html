Registration changes

Section: Account Maintenance · Page ID: account-registration-changes

Changing how an account is owned: adding or removing owners, retitling to a trust, and why most changes require a new account.

On this page

  • Common changes

What a registration is

The registration is the legal ownership of an account: who owns it and in what capacity (individual, joint, custodian, trustee, IRA owner). It determines who can act, how it is taxed, and what happens at death.

Common changes

ChangeHow it is done
Individual to joint (add a spouse)Open a joint account; transfer shares in kind from the individual account by journal
Joint to individual (remove an owner)All owners sign a transfer instruction (or provide a court order)
Individual to trustOpen a trust account; transfer shares in kind. See Retitling accounts to a trust
Custodial to the former minorAt the age of termination. See When a custodial account's minor comes of age
Joint to survivor after deathDeath certificate. See Surviving joint owners
IRA to another personNot possible except in divorce (transfer incident to divorce) or death. See Divorce and account transfers

Taxes

Moving shares in kind between registrations with the same tax owner (for example, individual to a revocable trust using the same SSN) is not a sale. Moving shares to a different person is a gift and not a sale either, but gift-tax rules may apply; refer the client to a tax professional. Selling and repurchasing would be a taxable sale, so in-kind transfer is the norm.

In chat

Registration changes are not chat tasks because they need signatures and often documents. Explain the process, send the client the Account Registration Change form, and route to Account Services if the client has questions about documents.
