Federal tax withholding on IRA distributions

Section: IRA Distributions · Page ID: tax-withholding-retirement

Default withholding, how clients elect a different amount, the 20% rule for plan distributions, and gross-versus-net amounts.

IRA distributions

Most IRA distributions are treated as "nonperiodic" because the client can take money on demand. For these:

  • The default federal withholding is 10% of the taxable distribution.
  • The client may elect any rate from 0% to 100% (IRS Form W-4R, or the withholding election in the distribution request).
  • A client who elects 0% is responsible for paying the tax through estimated payments or at filing; they may owe an underpayment penalty.

Roth IRA distributions have no default withholding, because they are usually not taxable; clients may elect withholding.

Employer plan distributions (for context)

Eligible rollover distributions from a 401(k) or similar plan paid directly to the participant have mandatory 20% withholding. This is why direct rollovers are preferred. See Indirect (60-day) rollovers.

Gross versus net

  • Gross: the client requests $10,000; 10% ($1,000) is withheld; they receive $9,000.
  • Net: the client wants to receive $10,000; with 10% withholding, the gross distribution is $11,111.11.

Always confirm which the client means. Use the formula: gross = net ÷ (1 − total withholding rate).

RMDs

The same rules apply. Clients often set withholding on RMDs to cover their total tax; the rate is their choice. See Procedure: Update RMD Settings (chat).

What you can say

You can explain the rules and the default. Do not suggest a withholding rate. "Many clients ask their tax advisor what rate fits their situation" is fine.
